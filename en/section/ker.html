Kerala medicine (Ayurveda)

The state of Kerala in southern India is one of the world's best-known centres of traditional Indian medicine, «Ayurveda», and many of its practitioners in the UAE come from there. Here is some of its history, what helps and what harms among its well-known treatments, and recipes from it that suit our homes.

Before any Ayurvedic treatment

  • Snakebite: go to hospital immediately for antivenom. Do not waste time with a traditional healer, and do not cut, suck or tightly tie the bite.
  • Do not stop medicines for diabetes, blood pressure, the heart or epilepsy for an Ayurvedic treatment.
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women and children: no Ayurvedic preparations without the doctor's approval.
  • Metallic «bhasma» preparations: do not take them, as heavy metals are added to them on purpose.
  • Treatment by vomiting, purging or enemas («panchakarma») is dangerous for older people and heart and kidney patients, and must only be done under medical supervision.

From the history of Kerala medicine

The book «Ashtanga Hridayam»

One of the best-known classical texts of Indian medicine, by Vagbhata

The book Kerala's physicians relied on; they used to learn its 7,120 verses by heart.

The eight physicians, the «Ashtavaidyas»

Hereditary families of Kerala

Eight Namboothiri Brahmin families who mastered the eight branches of medicine; four of them still practise. They wrote books in Malayalam such as «Sahasrayogam» and «Chikitsamanjari».

«Hortus Malabaricus» (The Garden of Malabar)

1678–1703

An encyclopedia of plants in 12 volumes, with about 742 Kerala plants and 794 engravings. It was compiled by the Dutch governor Van Rheede, and the folk healer Itty Achudan dictated its knowledge in Malayalam. It also records the plant names in Arabic.

Arya Vaidya Sala in Kottakkal

1902

Founded by the physician P. S. Varier, it turned household remedies into medicines made in factories, and Kerala became a world centre of Ayurveda.

Kerala and the Arabs

Centuries of trade

From Kerala's Malabar coast, black pepper, cardamom, ginger and turmeric reached our kitchens with Arab traders. That is why Kerala's herbs are close to the herbs of our own homes.

Kerala's well-known treatments: what helps and what harms

TreatmentIn KeralaWhat we know today
Coconut oil for dry skin and eczema
Sound		Used on the skin in Kerala since ancient times.In a trial of 117 children with mild to moderate eczema, 93% improved with virgin coconut oil versus 53% with mineral oil after 8 weeks. Stop if you are allergic to it.
«Kanji» rice gruel
Sound		Food for the sick, for convalescence and for the rainy season.A light food that replaces fluids and rests the stomach. With severe diarrhoea, start with oral rehydration solution first.
Spices in food: turmeric, pepper, curry leaves and ginger
Sound		The basis of Kerala cooking.Safe in food amounts; concentrated capsules are another matter, with their own warnings.
Coconut oil for the hair
Sound		Applied to the hair before washing.Reduces hair breakage in laboratory studies, but does not treat hair loss caused by illness.
Warm oil massage «abhyanga», and «shirodhara», pouring oil on the forehead
With care		Among Kerala's best-known treatments.Relaxing and eases stress, but studies are few and small. Beware of slipping afterwards and of hot oil, and do not apply it to inflamed or broken skin.
«Karkidakam kanji» medicinal gruel
With care		In the rainy month (mid-July to mid-August), rice is cooked with a herbal mix.Mixes vary from home to home, so ask about the ingredients. Pregnant women should not take it without the doctor.
Oil pulling «gandusha»
With care		Swishing coconut or sesame oil in the mouth for a few minutes each morning.No reliable studies show that it prevents tooth decay, and the American Dental Association does not recommend it. It does not replace brushing with fluoride toothpaste.
Chyawanprash» paste
With care		A jam of amla with ghee, honey and herbs, taken for immunity.Evidence is limited, and it is high in sugar, so people with diabetes should be careful.
Metallic «bhasma» preparations (rasa shastra)
Harmful		Metals are added on purpose as «medicine».In a US study (2008), lead, mercury or arsenic was found in about 21% of Ayurvedic preparations bought online, and the metallic ones were twice as likely to contain them. Cases of lead poisoning have been reported.
Snakebite treatment by a traditional healer
Harmful		Toxicology («visha chikitsa») is one of the best-known branches of Kerala folk medicine.It delays antivenom, the only treatment that saves a person from the venom. In India about 58,000 people die of snakebite every year, half of them in the rainy season.
«Panchakarma» without supervision
Harmful		Vomiting, purging and enemas «to purify the body».Can cause dehydration and salt imbalance, and is most dangerous for older people, heart and kidney patients and pregnant women.

If you go to an Ayurveda centre in the UAE

  • • No one may practise Ayurveda in the UAE without a licence from the health authority: the Ministry of Health, the Dubai Health Authority or the Abu Dhabi Department of Health. Ask for the licence number.
  • • Do not get treated by someone working from home or from an unlicensed shop, and do not buy «Ayurvedic medicines» on social media.
  • • Tell your doctor about every preparation you take, as some interact with medicines.
  • • Beware of anyone who promises a complete cure for diabetes, cancer or infertility.
