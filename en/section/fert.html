Delayed pregnancy and fertility

About one in six people of reproductive age experience delayed pregnancy; it is common and often treatable. Here: when to see a doctor, causes of delay in men and women, what science says about date palm pollen and other herbs, and myths that can delay pregnancy further.

See a doctor immediately

  • Severe pain on one side of the lower abdomen with a late period, bleeding or dizziness: this may be an ectopic pregnancy, which is an emergency.
  • Severe sudden pain in a testicle: an emergency.

When to see a doctor

  • After a year of regular trying without pregnancy, if the wife is under 35.
  • After 6 months if the wife is 35 to 39, and immediately after forty.
  • Without waiting if periods are irregular or absent, or very painful, or there was a previous pelvic infection or abdominal surgery, or known polycystic ovaries or endometriosis.
  • Without waiting, for the man if there was previous surgery or injury to the testicles, an undescended testicle in childhood, chemotherapy, erection or ejaculation problems, or use of gym hormones.

Both partners are examined together from the start. A semen test, hormone tests and imaging of the womb and ovaries are simple first tests.

Causes of delay

In women: ovulation problems

The commonest cause, most often polycystic ovary syndrome, sometimes with irregular periods, acne and excess hair. It can also come from the thyroid, the milk hormone (prolactin), severe thinness or obesity.

In women: fallopian tubes and womb

Blocked tubes after infection or surgery, endometriosis, and fibroids and polyps in the womb.

In women: age

Fertility declines gradually after thirty, and clearly after 35, as the number and quality of eggs fall.

In men: semen

A low count, poor motility or abnormal shape, which only shows on testing; a man can look completely healthy.

In men: varicocele and heat

Varicocele is common and treatable. Heat is harmful: long sitting, hot baths and saunas, a laptop on the thighs, and the heat of outdoor work.

In men: hormones and smoking

Testosterone supplements and steroids sold in gyms stop sperm production. Smoking, shisha and medwakh weaken semen.

Unknown cause

In about a quarter of cases, both partners' tests are normal. Many of them conceive with time or simple treatment.

What actually helps

  • Relatively strong Intercourse every two to three days throughout the month. Waiting only for ovulation day wastes chances and adds stress.
  • Relatively strong A healthy weight for both partners. Obesity and severe thinness harm ovulation and semen, and losing 5–10% of weight can restore ovulation in overweight women with polycystic ovaries.
  • Relatively strong Stopping smoking, shisha and medwakh for both partners.
  • Relatively strong Stopping testosterone supplements and steroids and telling the doctor about them, as semen may need months to recover.
  • Relatively strong For women before pregnancy: ask your doctor about folic acid starting three months before pregnancy, as it protects the baby from spinal defects.
  • Moderate Keeping heat away from the testicles: no laptop on the thighs, fewer hot baths and saunas, loose underwear, and get up from long sitting.
  • Moderate A Mediterranean diet: vegetables, fruit, legumes, fish, olive oil and nuts. In one trial, a daily handful of walnuts (75 g) improved semen in healthy young men.
  • Moderate Coffee in moderation: two cups a day at most, and less in pregnancy.
  • Limited Easing stress: the delay itself causes great pressure. Talking between the couple, family support and counselling help with patience and decisions.

Date palm pollen and herbs

With care		The best-known fertility remedy in the Gulf. In a 2024 Iranian trial of 80 men with weak semen, taking 6 g a day for 3 months, count and motility improved more than with a drug given at a low dose. In women there are small trials: it increased egg follicles less than ovulation drugs, and pregnancy rates did not differ much. The evidence is promising but limited. It is one of the commonest causes of allergy and asthma, and women should take it only with their doctor's approval.

An important rule: a woman trying to conceive may be in the first weeks of pregnancy without knowing, so she should not take regular herbs or supplements without her doctor's approval. Anyone receiving fertility treatment should tell their doctor about every herb.

Common myths

«Delay is always the woman's fault»

A male factor is present in about half of cases. A semen test is one of the first tests, and there is nothing shameful in it for the man.

«Herbal pessaries cleanse the womb»

Mixtures placed in the vagina with musk, alum and herbs can cause burns, infections and blocked tubes, delaying pregnancy further.

«Herbal steaming of the womb»

There is no evidence it helps, and it has caused burns in the intimate area.

«Raising the legs after intercourse helps pregnancy»

It does no harm, but it does not raise the chance; sperm reach the cervix within minutes.

«Testosterone supplements boost fertility»

Quite the opposite: they stop sperm production.

«Past birth control pills caused infertility»

Fertility usually returns within months of stopping them. The contraceptive injection, however, may take up to a year.

«Date palm pollen or royal jelly guarantees pregnancy»

No herb guarantees pregnancy. Anyone selling a «guaranteed fertility mix» is selling an illusion, and it may contain hidden hormones or drugs.

«Travel and relax and pregnancy will come»

Rest is good, but stress is usually not the main cause, and delay wastes precious time, especially after 35.
