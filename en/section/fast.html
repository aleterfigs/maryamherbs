Fasting and health

Fasting is worship before it is a treatment, and «intermittent fasting» has become one of the most popular ways to lose weight. Here is what science says about it, Ramadan fasting with chronic diseases, a healthy iftar and suhoor, voluntary fasts with hadith grades, and common myths.

When to break your fast at once

  • Low blood sugar: shaking, sweating, intense hunger, dizziness and confusion, or a reading below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L): break your fast at once with juice, dates or a spoonful of honey.
  • Very high blood sugar: above 300 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L), or thirst, frequent urination and vomiting: break your fast and call your doctor.
  • Fainting, confusion, chest pain or shortness of breath: break your fast and call an ambulance (998 in the UAE, or your local ambulance number if you are elsewhere).
  • Severe dehydration: dizziness on standing, and very little, very dark urine, especially in summer heat and when working outdoors.
  • Repeated vomiting or diarrhoea, or a high fever.

Intermittent fasting: what science says

  • Relatively strong Intermittent fasting does reduce weight, by about as much as ordinary daily calorie reduction. In an analysis of 99 trials with more than 6,500 people, published in the BMJ in 2025, all intermittent fasting methods produced a small weight loss compared with eating without limits, and their results were close to calorie reduction. So it is a method that suits some people, not a miracle.
  • Moderate Its forms: limiting eating to 8 to 10 hours a day (16:8), fasting two days a week (5:2), or fasting one day and eating the next. The last resembles the fast of Dawud in the Sunnah.
  • Moderate Blood sugar, fats and blood pressure: a moderate improvement similar to the effect of the weight loss itself; the long-term effect is not yet known.
  • Limited «Cell renewal» (autophagy): the Japanese scientist Ohsumi won the 2016 Nobel Prize for discovering its mechanism in yeast. Claims that fasting activates it and so cures diseases have not been proven in humans.
  • Moderate Ramadan fasting: usually reduces weight by about one kilogram, which mostly returns after Ramadan if eating goes back to how it was. Blood sugar and fats may improve in those who eat moderately at iftar.

Who should not fast to lose weight or as treatment

  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women: no intermittent fasting to lose weight. In Ramadan she consults her doctor, and she has the religious concession.
  • Growing children and teenagers: no fasting to lose weight.
  • Anyone who has or has had an eating disorder, such as anorexia or binge eating.
  • People with diabetes on insulin or sulfonylurea tablets, unless supervised by a doctor, because of the risk of low blood sugar.
  • People with advanced kidney disease, unstable heart disease, the very thin, and frail older people.
  • People with gout or gallstones: long fasts may trigger them.

Ramadan fasting and chronic diseases

Diabetes

The international Diabetes and Ramadan guidelines divide patients into risk levels according to the type of diabetes, its medicines and its complications. Those at very high risk are advised not to fast, such as people with repeated or unnoticed low blood sugar, uncontrolled type 1 diabetes, and pregnant women with diabetes. Those at low risk can usually fast while checking their blood sugar. The decision is made with the doctor weeks before Ramadan, who may change the timing or type of medicine. Most scholars hold that a finger-prick sugar test does not break the fast.

Blood pressure and heart

Most people with controlled blood pressure can fast, and a once-daily medicine is moved to iftar or suhoor with the doctor's approval. Lots of salt and pickles at suhoor raise blood pressure and increase thirst.

Kidneys and stones

Dehydration in the Gulf heat raises the risk of stones. Drink water spread out between iftar and suhoor: two glasses at iftar, then a glass every hour. People with chronic kidney disease consult their doctor before fasting.

Heartburn and headache

A heavy iftar followed by sleep increases heartburn. Headaches in the first days usually come from giving up coffee and from dehydration: cut down on coffee two weeks before Ramadan.

Medicines

Do not stop a medicine on your own. The doctor changes the timing or chooses a once-daily medicine. As for inhalers, eye drops, injections and the like, ask scholars about their ruling in fasting.

A sick person harmed by fasting has a religious concession, and their reward is preserved, God willing. The doctor assesses the health condition, and a mufti is asked about the ruling.

A healthy iftar and suhoor

  • Relatively strong Start iftar with dates and water as in the Sunnah: quick sugar that restores energy, and water that makes up for dehydration. Then pray Maghrib, then continue with a moderate meal.
  • Relatively strong A late suhoor just before dawn, with protein and fibre: eggs, fava beans or chickpeas, yoghurt, oats or brown bread, vegetables. It keeps you full longer and reduces tiredness.
  • Moderate Water between iftar and suhoor: about 8 glasses spread out, not all at once. Plenty of watery fruit and vegetables such as watermelon and cucumber.
  • Moderate Cut down on fried foods such as samosas and luqaimat, sweets soaked in sugar and syrup, sweetened juices and fizzy drinks, and salty food at suhoor.
  • Moderate A light walk an hour or two after iftar, or the Taraweeh prayer itself, helps digestion and blood sugar.

Common myths

«Fasting cures cancer»

There is no evidence that fasting cures cancer. Fasting during chemotherapy is being studied in trials and is only done under the oncologist's supervision, as it may harm a weak patient.

«A water fast for days purifies the body of toxins»

The liver and kidneys clean the body every day. A long water-only fast may cause salt imbalances, low blood sugar and gout attacks, and danger when eating is suddenly resumed.

«A juice fast (detox) cleans the body»

Juices are a lot of sugar with no fibre or protein, and they clean nothing. Some of their laxative blends harm the kidneys.

«People with diabetes can fast and leave their medicine»

Stopping a medicine or taking it at the wrong time can cause dangerous lows or highs. The doctor adjusts the dose and timing.

«Breaking the fast with cold water harms the stomach»

There is no evidence for this. Water and dates are a good start; what matters is not filling the stomach all at once.

«Drinking a lot of water at suhoor is enough for the whole day»

The body gets rid of the excess quickly. Better to drink water spread out from iftar to suhoor.
