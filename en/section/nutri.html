Food & vitamins

Foods that make up for the most common deficiencies. Symptoms have many causes, and a test at the doctor's is what confirms a deficiency and decides whether supplements are needed.

Search by symptom

Iron

Daily need: 8 mg for men, 18 mg for women before menopause, and 27 mg in pregnancy

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Women with heavy periods
  • Pregnant women
  • Children and teenagers during growth spurts
  • Vegetarians
  • People who drink tea with meals

Deficiency symptoms

  • Tiredness and exhaustion
  • Paleness
  • Shortness of breath on exertion
  • Dizziness
  • Hair loss
  • Brittle or spoon-shaped nails
  • Cravings to eat ice or clay

Best sources

  • ExcellentLiver and red meat (easily absorbed iron) Liver Lamb and mutton Camel meat
  • GoodChicken, fish and shellfish Chicken and chicken broth
  • GoodLentils, chickpeas, fava beans and black-eyed peas
  • ModerateSpinach, molokhia and mallow
  • ModerateDate syrup, dried dates and dried apricots
  • ModeratePumpkin seeds, sesame and tahini

For better absorption

  • Eat with a source of vitamin C (lemon, tomato, pepper, orange) to double the absorption of plant iron.
  • Drink tea and coffee an hour after meals, not with them.
  • Milk and calcium reduce iron absorption when taken together.
Adult iron tablets can be deadly for children if swallowed: keep them out of reach. And do not take iron without a test, because too much is harmful.

Protein

Daily need: About 0.8 g per kilo of body weight a day for adults, and a little more for older people, pregnant and breastfeeding women, and people recovering from illness

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Older people, especially those with a poor appetite
  • People recovering from illness or surgery
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women
  • Children during growth spurts
  • Vegetarians who do not vary their pulses and grains
  • People on strict weight-loss diets

Deficiency symptoms

  • Muscle weakness and loss of muscle mass
  • Tiredness
  • Slow wound healing
  • Hair loss and weak nails
  • Frequent infections
  • Swollen feet in severe deficiency

Best sources

  • ExcellentEggs Eggs
  • ExcellentChicken, meat and fish Chicken and chicken broth Lamb and mutton Camel meat
  • GoodYogurt, laban, milk and cheese Yogurt and laban Milk
  • GoodLentils, chickpeas, broad beans and beans
  • ModerateNuts and seeds: almonds, pumpkin seeds and sesame

For better absorption

  • Spread protein across your three meals rather than having it all at once.
  • For vegetarians: combine pulses with grains, such as lentils with rice or chickpeas with bread.
  • For older people: an egg at breakfast and yogurt between meals raise protein easily.
People with kidney disease should only increase protein under a doctor's supervision. Most people do not need protein powders, and some are adulterated with banned substances.

Vitamin D

Daily need: 600 IU (15 mcg) daily for adults, and 800 for those over seventy

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Most people in the Gulf despite the sun, because of the heat, staying indoors and covering the body
  • People with dark skin
  • Older people
  • Exclusively breastfed babies
  • People with obesity

Deficiency symptoms

  • Bone and muscle pain
  • Tiredness
  • Muscle weakness
  • Frequent infections
  • Hair loss

Best sources

  • GoodOily fish: salmon, sardines, mackerel (kingfish) and tuna
  • ModerateEgg yolk Eggs
  • ModerateFortified milk, laban and juices Milk Yogurt and laban
  • LowSun-exposed mushrooms
  • The main sourceSun: 10–30 minutes on the arms and face a few times a week, before the skin reddens

For better absorption

  • Food alone rarely provides enough; many people need a supplement after a blood test.
  • Absorbed better with a meal containing fat.
  • Window glass blocks the rays that make vitamin D.
Very high doses over long periods without monitoring can raise blood calcium and harm the kidneys. Set the dose with the doctor based on your test.

Vitamin B12

Daily need: 2.4 mcg daily for adults

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Vegans who eat no animal products
  • Older people
  • People taking metformin for diabetes
  • People taking acid medicines for long periods
  • After stomach operations and sleeve surgery

Deficiency symptoms

  • Numbness and tingling in the hands and feet
  • Tiredness and paleness
  • Poor memory and concentration
  • Mouth ulcers and a red tongue
  • Loss of balance

Best sources

  • ExcellentLiver and shellfish Liver
  • GoodMeat, fish, sardines and tuna
  • GoodEggs, milk, laban and cheese Eggs Milk Yogurt and laban
  • ModerateFortified foods (breakfast cereals)

For better absorption

  • Not found in plants in useful amounts.
  • With metformin or after stomach surgery: have it checked yearly.
  • Long-term deficiency can cause permanent nerve damage, so do not delay testing.
Treating a severe deficiency may need injections decided by the doctor.

Folic acid

Daily need: 400 mcg daily, and 600 in pregnancy

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Women planning pregnancy and pregnant women
  • People taking some epilepsy medicines
  • People who drink alcohol
  • Malnutrition

Deficiency symptoms

  • Tiredness and paleness (anaemia)
  • Mouth ulcers
  • Irritability

Best sources

  • GoodGreen leafy vegetables: spinach, molokhia, rocket and parsley
  • GoodLentils, chickpeas and fava beans
  • ModerateOranges and avocado
  • ModerateFortified bread and flour

For better absorption

  • Every woman who could become pregnant is advised to take folic acid tablets at least a month before pregnancy and in the early months, because it protects the baby from spinal defects.
  • Long cooking destroys some of it; eat some vegetables raw.
The usual preventive dose is safe, but high doses are set with the doctor.

Calcium

Daily need: 1000 mg daily for adults, 1,300 for teenagers, and 1,200 for women over fifty

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Teenagers
  • Women after menopause
  • People who do not eat dairy
  • Vitamin D deficiency

Deficiency symptoms

  • Muscle cramps
  • Tingling around the mouth and fingers
  • Osteoporosis in the long run (usually without symptoms)

Best sources

  • ExcellentMilk, laban, yoghurt and cheese Milk Yogurt and laban Goat milk
  • GoodTinned sardines with their bones
  • GoodTahini and sesame
  • ModerateAlmonds and dried figs Fig
  • ModerateLeafy greens (molokhia, rocket and broccoli)

For better absorption

  • Needs vitamin D to be absorbed.
  • The body absorbs calcium better in small amounts through the day.
  • Fizzy drinks and too much salt and coffee reduce calcium stores.
Too many calcium supplements may cause kidney stones and constipation; food is the best source.

Zinc

Daily need: 11 mg for men, and 8 mg for women

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Vegetarians
  • Older people
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women
  • Chronic bowel diseases

Deficiency symptoms

  • Hair loss
  • Slow wound healing
  • Frequent infections
  • Weak sense of taste and smell
  • Skin rash

Best sources

  • ExcellentOysters
  • GoodRed meat and chicken Lamb and mutton Chicken and chicken broth
  • GoodPumpkin seeds, sesame and cashews
  • ModerateChickpeas, lentils and fava beans
  • ModerateOats and whole grains

For better absorption

  • Soaking and sprouting improve its absorption from pulses and grains.
Too many zinc supplements over long periods cause copper deficiency and anaemia.

Magnesium

Daily need: 400–420 mg for men, and 310–320 mg for women

Most at risk of deficiency

  • People with diabetes
  • People taking diuretics or acid medicines for long periods
  • Older people

Deficiency symptoms

  • Muscle cramps, especially at night
  • Tiredness
  • Palpitations
  • Headache
  • Trouble sleeping

Best sources

  • GoodNuts: almonds and cashews
  • GoodSeeds: pumpkin, sesame and flax
  • GoodWhole grains and oats
  • ModerateLeafy greens and pulses
  • ModerateDates, bananas and dark chocolate

For better absorption

  • Whole grains are much richer than white.
Large doses of magnesium supplements cause diarrhoea, and kidney patients should be careful with them.

Iodine

Daily need: 150 mcg for adults, 220 in pregnancy, and 290 when breastfeeding

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women
  • People using non-iodised salt

Deficiency symptoms

  • An enlarged thyroid
  • Tiredness, weight gain and feeling cold (underactive thyroid)
  • In pregnancy: affects the baby's brain development

Best sources

  • ExcellentFish and seafood
  • GoodIodised salt
  • GoodMilk, laban and eggs Milk Yogurt and laban Eggs

For better absorption

  • Use iodised salt at home, in moderate amounts.
  • Himalayan salt and sea salt are usually not iodised.
Do not take iodine supplements or concentrated seaweed without a doctor, especially with thyroid disease.

Vitamin C

Daily need: 90 mg for men, and 75 mg for women, plus 35 mg for smokers

Most at risk of deficiency

  • People who do not eat fresh fruit and vegetables
  • Smokers
  • Older people

Deficiency symptoms

  • Bleeding gums
  • Slow wound healing
  • Bruising easily
  • Tiredness

Best sources

  • ExcellentGuava, sweet pepper and kiwi
  • ExcellentAmla, baobab and rose hips
  • GoodOranges, lemons and strawberries
  • GoodBroccoli and tomatoes

For better absorption

  • Heat and long storage destroy it, so fresh is best.
  • Doubles iron absorption from plants.
Very large supplement doses may cause diarrhoea and kidney stones.

Vitamin A

Daily need: 900 mcg for men, and 700 mcg for women

Most at risk of deficiency

  • Malnutrition
  • Fat-absorption disorders

Deficiency symptoms

  • Poor vision in the dark
  • Dry eyes and skin
  • Frequent infections

Best sources

  • ExcellentLiver (in moderate amounts) Liver
  • GoodCarrots, sweet potatoes, pumpkin and mango
  • GoodSpinach, molokhia and moringa leaves
  • ModerateEggs, milk and butter Eggs Milk Ghee

For better absorption

  • Colourful plant sources are safe and do not cause excess.
Pregnant women should not eat much liver or take vitamin A supplements beyond their pregnancy vitamins, because excess harms the baby.

Omega-3

Daily need: Two portions of oily fish a week

Most at risk of deficiency

  • People who do not eat fish

Deficiency symptoms

  • No clear symptoms of deficiency, but it matters for heart, brain and eye health

Best sources

  • ExcellentSardines, mackerel (kingfish), salmon and tuna
  • Good (plant)Flaxseed, walnuts and chia seeds

For better absorption

  • Only a small part of plant omega-3 is converted into the form the body uses.
High-dose fish-oil supplements may increase bleeding with blood thinners.

When do you need a test and a doctor?

  • Tiredness, paleness or shortness of breath lasting more than two weeks.
  • Numbness in the limbs or poor memory.
  • Before and during pregnancy, and with heavy periods.
  • With long-term medicines such as metformin and acid medicines, or after stomach surgery.
  • Do not take «vitamin injections» or high doses without a test; too much of some vitamins is as harmful as too little.
