Dedication

This guide is an ongoing charity for the soul of my mother Maryam , may God have mercy on her; I have named it after her.

O God, forgive her and have mercy on her, grant her well-being and pardon her, make her resting place honourable and her entrance wide, wash her with water, snow and hail, cleanse her of sins as a white garment is cleansed of dirt, make her grave one of the gardens of Paradise, and place this work in the scale of her good deeds.

How it was made

  • The herbs were chosen because they are common in Arab homes and markets, and then the best-known herbs of other traditions were added.
  • The content was prepared with the help of artificial intelligence (Claude, by Anthropic), then checked against scientific and official sources listed under each herb, including the Egyptian Drug Authority, the European Medicines Agency and the US National Institutes of Health. If you find a mistake, tell us with the button under each herb and blend, and we will correct it.
  • For the wild plants of the UAE we rely on the research of the Fujairah Research Centre published in peer-reviewed scientific journals, and on the Encyclopedia of Medicinal Plants of the Department of Health – Abu Dhabi.
  • For each herb: its uses, the level of scientific evidence, how to prepare it, its cautions and its drug interactions.
  • The blends are traditional and home recipes; scientific evidence is usually about each herb on its own, and the blend itself has not been tested as a combination.
  • Prophetic-medicine hadiths are cited with their sources and grading, and those that are not authentic are flagged.
  • Andalusian and Arab-medicine recipes are inspired by their books, with quantities adapted for the modern kitchen.
  • The assistant and safety checker rely on written rules for each herb, excluding whatever conflicts with pregnancy, illnesses, medicines and allergies.

Evidence levels

  • Relatively strong Multiple clinical trials in humans with consistent results.
  • Moderate Human trials, but small or with mixed results.
  • Limited Mostly traditional use and lab or animal studies.
  • Unsafe Its risks outweigh any possible benefit; we do not recommend it.

How is a blend's reliability rated?

Each blend has a three-part badge. It is an estimated rating of the blend's ingredients, because most blends have not been tested as a complete combination.

  • Evidence (dots): we take the strongest herb in the blend for the same problem the blend is used for. Three dots: the herb is well studied for this exact problem. Two dots: moderate studies, or a herb with strong evidence but for another use. One dot: limited evidence. No dots: traditional with no studies.
  • Safety: Safe for most people: no common contraindications for its ingredients. Needs caution: contains an ingredient forbidden in pregnancy, breastfeeding, or with a particular illness or medicine. Short-term or supervised use: contains a potent ingredient such as senna, liquorice or valerian.
  • Source: a hadith or report, classical medical books, an inherited folk custom, or a modern home recipe. The source shows the origin, not the effectiveness.
  • The default order of blends is «Most reliable first»: strongest evidence, then safest. It can be changed to «By tradition».
  • «Suggest a blend» checks your own situation (age, pregnancy, illnesses, medicines and allergies), and is more precise than the general safety badge.

Note

This guide is for education and does not replace a doctor or pharmacist, and no prescribed medicine should be stopped because of it. Herbs can interact with medicines, and pregnant and breastfeeding women, children, older people and those with chronic illnesses are the people who most need advice. At any danger sign, go to the doctor or emergency department immediately.
