Sekanjabin

A vinegar-and-honey syrup, one of the best-known drinks of Ibn Sina and al-Razi for thirst and digestion.

Relatively strong evidenceSafe for most peopleClassical medical books
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Mint (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Classical medical books.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.

Ingredients (For two cups)

  • Honey: Two tablespoons of honey
  • One tablespoon of grape or apple vinegar
  • Two cups of cold water
  • Mint: Mint leaves (a Persian addition)

Method

  1. Dissolve the honey in a little warm water.
  2. Add the vinegar and the cold water.
  3. Serve with mint; it can be chilled.
How much and when? One cup once or twice a day; can be continued 4–6 weeks, then a week's break.
Acidic: caution with stomach ulcers and reflux. Diabetics count the honey.
