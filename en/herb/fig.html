Fig

Ficus carica

Fruit (and the sap of the branches)

Moderate

The fruit is a suitable food, cut up for little ones. Fig sap must never be put on a child's skin.

In Islamic heritage

Verse of the QuranGod says: «By the fig and the olive» [At-Tin: 1]. A hadith is narrated: «If I were to say a fruit came down from Paradise, I would say it is this… it cures piles and helps gout», but Ibn al-Qayyim cited it in Zad al-Ma'ad with doubts about its authenticity, so it cannot be relied on.

Common uses

  • Constipation: soaked dried figs, supported by small human trials
  • Food for the weak, the convalescent and children
  • Fig sap is used in folk medicine on warts and some skin conditions

Usual preparation

Eat fresh, or soak dried figs in water overnight and eat them on an empty stomach.

Cautions

  • Fig sap and leaves with sunlight cause burns, blisters and dark patches that can last for months; never use on children, and if tried, only on a small spot kept out of the sun
  • Dried figs are high in sugar: one or two for people with diabetes
  • Fig leaf may lower blood sugar, so do not drink it with diabetes medicines without your doctor
  • Cut up for young children to avoid choking

Possible drug interactions

  • Rich in vitamin K: people on warfarin should not change how much they eat suddenly
  • Fig leaf with diabetes medicines: risk of low blood sugar

Growing and propagation

Easy in the Gulf
Origin and climate
A Mediterranean tree that tolerates heat and drought
In the Gulf
Does well in the Gulf; best planted in winter
Light, water and soil
Full sun or light shade in summer, regular watering while fruiting, well-drained soil
Propagation
From hardwood cuttings in winter (they root easily) or nursery plants
Harvest
Fruit in summer; wear gloves when picking because the branch sap irritates the skin in sunlight
