In Islamic heritage

Verse of the QuranGod says: «By the fig and the olive» [At-Tin: 1]. A hadith is narrated: «If I were to say a fruit came down from Paradise, I would say it is this… it cures piles and helps gout», but Ibn al-Qayyim cited it in Zad al-Ma'ad with doubts about its authenticity, so it cannot be relied on.

Dried figs are among the richest fruits in fibre and calcium, but their sugar is concentrated: 3 dried figs equal a large piece of fruit in sugar.

What science says
  • Moderate Constipation and IBS: in a trial at Isfahan University with 150 patients with constipation-predominant IBS, participants ate 45 g of dried figs before breakfast and the same before lunch with a glass of water, 90 g a day, for about 4 months, compared with those who kept their usual diet. In a Korean trial, fig paste sped up bowel movement.
  • Relatively strong Fibre in general prevents constipation and benefits the heart, blood sugar and weight. This is established for fibre from all foods, and figs are among its richest sources.
  • Limited Warts: in a small trial of 25 patients, 44% of warts cleared with fig latex versus 56% with cryotherapy at the doctor's. But fig latex burns the skin in the sun.
  • Limited Fig leaves and blood sugar: small, older trials suggested they may lower blood sugar slightly, so they should not be combined with diabetes medicines without a doctor.
  • Limited Antioxidants: dark figs are rich in them in lab analyses, but their therapeutic effect in humans is unknown.
In the Quran and Sunnah
  • God swore by it: ﴿By the fig and the olive﴾ [At-Tin: 1], and a chapter of the Quran bears its name.
  • A hadith is narrated: «If I were to say a fruit came down from Paradise, I would say it is this… it cuts off haemorrhoids and helps gout»; Ibn al-Qayyim was uncertain of its authenticity and hadith scholars graded it weak, so it is not used as evidence.
How to choose and store it
  • Fresh: slightly soft when pressed, with no leaking cracks or fermented smell. Keep in the fridge and eat within two or three days.
  • Dried: even in colour with a sweet smell. Discard any fig that is mouldy or tastes bitter, as poorly dried or stored figs can grow fungi that produce toxins.
  • The white powder on dried figs is usually the fig's own sugar crystallised on the surface; it is smooth and tastes sweet. Mould is fuzzy and may be green or grey.
  • Keep dried figs in an airtight container in a cool, dry place or in the fridge.
Myths
  • «Every fig contains a dead wasp»: this is true of some varieties that need pollination by the fig wasp, and the fruit's enzymes digest it so nothing remains. Most home fig varieties bear fruit without pollination at all.
  • «Fig latex is a safe skin remedy»: it contains substances that make the skin burn in the sun, and burns and dark patches lasting months have been recorded. Individual stories of cure are not enough evidence.
  • «Dried figs are fine for diabetics without limit»: their sugar is concentrated, so one or two with a meal is enough.
Growing it in the UAE
  • A tree that tolerates heat and sandy soil, and succeeds in the UAE with regular watering.
  • Sowing: from cuttings in winter, December to February, when the tree is dormant.
  • Watering: deep and regular, especially in summer and when fruit is ripening, without waterlogging the soil.
  • Pruning: in winter, to open up the tree and encourage fruiting branches.
  • Fruit: mostly in early summer, and some varieties give a second crop later.
  • Pests: birds, mites in dry heat, rust on the leaves, and scale insects.
  • Wear gloves and long sleeves when pruning and picking, as the branches' latex burns the skin in the sun.

Common uses

  • Constipation: dried figs daily, supported by human trials
  • A plant source of fibre, calcium, potassium and iron
  • Food for the weak, people recovering from illness and children
  • Fig latex is used traditionally on warts, with a risk of burns

Usual preparation

Eat fresh, or soak dried figs in water overnight and eat them on an empty stomach.

Cautions

  • Fig sap and leaves with sunlight cause burns, blisters and dark patches that can last for months; never use on children, and if tried, only on a small spot kept out of the sun
  • Dried figs are high in sugar: one or two for people with diabetes
  • Fig leaf may lower blood sugar, so do not drink it with diabetes medicines without your doctor
  • Cut up for young children to avoid choking

Possible drug interactions

  • Rich in vitamin K: people on warfarin should not change how much they eat suddenly
  • Fig leaf with diabetes medicines: risk of low blood sugar

Where to find it

  • Supermarkets and grocers
  • Plant nurseries (seeds or seedlings), to grow it at home

Ask for it by its names: التينة، الكرموس، الحماط، البلس. Its scientific name Ficus carica helps with pharmacists and foreign shops.

Choose a sealed pack with a packing date, and whole herbs rather than ground if possible, as ground herbs lose their aroma and are easier to adulterate. Bright colour and a strong aroma are signs of quality. Store in an airtight container away from light and moisture.

For growing: choose freshly packed seeds, or a healthy green seedling without spots or insects. How to grow it is in the growing section above.

Beware of «guaranteed mixes», products without a label or ingredients, and unknown online products; some have been found to contain hidden cortisone or drugs. We do not name shops so that the guide stays neutral.
