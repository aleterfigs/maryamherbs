Ginger

Zingiber officinale
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
الجنزبيل · Ginger
Part used
Root (rhizome)
Strength of evidence
Relatively strong
For children
For travel sickness from age 6, in a small amount.
NauseaDigestion & bloatingPain & inflammationColds & coughPostpartumHeadache

In Islamic heritage

Verse of the QuranMentioned in the Quran among the drinks of the people of Paradise: “And they will be given to drink a cup whose mixture is ginger” [Quran 76:17].

Common uses

  • Nausea and travel sickness
  • Pregnancy nausea (after asking the doctor)
  • Period pain
  • Mild joint pain

Usual preparation

Infusion: thin slices of fresh root in boiling water for 10 minutes; lemon and honey can be added.

Cautions

  • May cause heartburn or an upset stomach in large amounts
  • Stop two weeks before surgery

Possible drug interactions

  • Blood thinners and antiplatelet drugs
  • Diabetes medicines (may lower blood sugar)

Growing and propagation

Possible with care
Origin and climate
A tropical rhizome that likes heat, humidity and shade
In the Gulf
Planted in spring (February–March) in a pot in the shade; needs humidity in summer
Light, water and soil
Part shade, regular watering without flooding, rich light soil
Propagation
By rhizome: choose a piece of shop ginger with «eyes» (buds), soak it overnight, and plant 3 cm deep with the bud facing up
Harvest
After 8–10 months when the leaves yellow, or cut a small piece from the side and leave the rest to grow
🌡️ Ideal temperaturefrom 22 to 30 °CStruggles above 38°C · harmed below 12°C
🧂 Tolerance of salty waterLowFresh or desalinated water (under about 1,000 ppm). Salty well water burns the leaf tips.
🧪 Soil acidity (pH)from 5.5 to 6.5Gulf soil is alkaline (7.5–8.5): mix in organic compost and a little agricultural sulphur, or grow it in a pot with ready-made potting mix.
💧 WateringWinter: Every 3–4 daysSummer: Every two days, misting the leaves
Life cycle
Perennial (rhizome)
Where to grow
A wide, shallow 35 cm pot
First harvest
8–10 months
Common pests
Rhizome rot from overwatering, red spider mite
