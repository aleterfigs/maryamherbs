Damask rose and rose water

Rosa damascena
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
الورد الجوري، الورد الطائفي، ماء الورد · Damask rose
Part used
Petals
Strength of evidence
Limited
For children
A few drops of food-grade rose water in food.
Sleep & anxietySkin & woundsDigestion & bloating

Common uses

  • Calming, refreshing drinks
  • Soothing the skin
  • Flavouring for food

Usual preparation

A few drops of food-grade rose water in water or milk, or an infusion of dried petals.

Cautions

  • For drinking, use only food-grade rose water, not cosmetic
  • Rare allergy

Possible drug interactions

  • No known interactions

Growing and propagation

Possible with care
Origin and climate
A shrub of temperate regions (Taif and Damascus)
In the Gulf
Does well in winter in the Gulf and struggles in summer; the Taif rose suits the highlands
Light, water and soil
Morning sun, regular watering, soil rich in compost
Propagation
By cuttings in autumn or nursery plants
Harvest
The flowers in the early morning as they open
🌡️ Ideal temperaturefrom 15 to 28 °CStruggles above 38°C · harmed below -5°C
🧂 Tolerance of salty waterLowFresh or desalinated water (under about 1,000 ppm). Salty well water burns the leaf tips.
🧪 Soil acidity (pH)from 6 to 7Gulf soil is alkaline (7.5–8.5): mix in organic compost and a little agricultural sulphur, or grow it in a pot with ready-made potting mix.
💧 WateringWinter: Every 2–3 daysSummer: Daily, in the morning
Life cycle
Shrub
Where to grow
A 40 cm pot or the ground
First harvest
3–4 months from the plant
Common pests
Aphids, red spider mite, powdery mildew
