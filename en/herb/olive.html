In Islamic heritage

Authenticity disputedThe Prophet ﷺ said: «Eat olive oil and anoint yourselves with it, for it is from a blessed tree» (narrated by Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah; hadith scholars differed on it: al-Albani graded it sahih and Tirmidhi pointed out a defect).

Grades of olive oil
GradeHow it is madeWhat it suits
Extra virginMechanically cold-pressed without chemicals, acidity 0.8% or less, and no taste defectsThe richest in beneficial compounds, and the one studied in heart research. For eating and cooking
VirginMechanically pressed, acidity up to 2%, with slight taste defectsFor cooking
«Olive oil» or «pure» olive oilA blend of oil refined with heat and chemicals and a little virgin oilMild taste, and far fewer benefits
Olive pomace oilExtracted from pressing residues with solvents, then refinedThe cheapest type, and it may not be called virgin olive oil

«First pressing» is an old marketing phrase, since modern oil is pressed only once. What matters is the words «extra virgin» and the harvest date.

In the Quran and Sunnah
  • Allah says: ﴿Lit from a blessed olive tree, neither of the east nor of the west, whose oil would almost glow﴾ [An-Nur: 35].
  • And Allah says: ﴿By the fig and the olive﴾ [At-Tin: 1].
  • «Eat olive oil and anoint yourselves with it, for it is from a blessed tree» (narrated by Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah). Disputed Al-Albani graded it sahih through its combined chains, while Tirmidhi pointed out an inconsistency in its chain.
What does the science say?
  • Relatively strong Heart and arteries: In the Spanish PREDIMED trial of about 7,400 people at risk of heart disease, a Mediterranean diet with extra virgin olive oil (about 4 tablespoons a day) reduced heart attacks, strokes and deaths from them by about 30%, compared with a low-fat diet.
  • Moderate Cholesterol: The European Food Safety Authority recognises that the polyphenols in virgin oil protect blood lipids from oxidation, provided the oil contains enough of them.
  • Moderate Instead of saturated fat: Replacing ghee and butter with olive oil lowers the risk of heart disease.
  • Limited Constipation: A spoonful on an empty stomach is a folk habit, and studies on it are few.
  • The benefit comes from a whole diet, not from a spoonful of oil with unhealthy food.
In the kitchen
  • Cooking and frying: Extra virgin olive oil withstands the heat of home cooking and frying, because most of its fats are monounsaturated and it contains antioxidants. But do not reuse frying oil many times.
  • Calories: A tablespoon has about 120 calories, so be moderate if you want to lose weight.
  • On food: The best way to enjoy its flavour and compounds, on salad, hummus, labneh and bread.
For skin, hair and ears
  • Dry skin and hair: A good moisturiser for many people.
  • Massaging newborns: Not recommended: British studies found olive oil may weaken the skin barrier of babies, especially those with eczema in the family. Sunflower oil or creams made for babies are more suitable.
  • Ear wax: Warm (not hot) drops of olive oil soften ear wax; do not use them with a perforated eardrum, pain or discharge.
How to tell genuine from fake?

Olive oil is one of the most adulterated foods in the world. In a 2010 University of California, Davis study, 69% of imported «extra virgin» samples failed quality tests. The commonest frauds are mixing it with cheap oils such as sunflower, soybean and hazelnut, selling refined or old oil as extra virgin, or dyeing it to look green.

Home tests that do not detect fraud:

  • The fridge test: Solidifying does not prove purity: some genuine oils do not solidify and some fake ones do. The University of California showed it is unreliable.
  • Colour: Green is not a sign of quality, and oil is sometimes dyed. Professional tasters use blue glasses so the colour does not mislead them.
  • The burning or density test: Neither detects fraud.

What really helps:

  • Taste: Fresh virgin oil smells of green olives or grass, with a slight bitterness and a peppery catch in the throat. These are signs of quality, not defects. A smell of wax, crayons or rust is a sign of old, rancid oil.
  • Harvest date: Choose the latest season's oil; it is best used within 18 months of harvest.
  • The container: Dark glass or a metal tin, not clear glass.
  • The label: The clear words «extra virgin», the country of origin, the producer's name, and the acidity if given.
  • Price: Extra virgin olive oil is costly to produce, and a very low price is a warning sign.
  • Source: From a mill or farmer you know, or a trusted brand with quality certificates.
  • The only proof: Laboratory analysis is the only way to detect blended oils and the oil's true grade.
Storage
  • Away from light and heat, not next to the stove or in the car.
  • Close the bottle well after each use, as air spoils the oil.
  • Use it within a few months of opening.
  • Very large containers spoil before they are finished if the family is small.
Common myths
  • «Olive oil is not suitable for frying»: Not true; extra virgin oil withstands home cooking heat.
  • «The greener the better»: Colour does not indicate quality.
  • «Bitterness and pepperiness are defects»: They are signs of fresh oil rich in beneficial compounds.
  • «Old oil is better, like honey»: Oil loses its flavour and compounds over time and then goes rancid.
  • «A spoonful on an empty stomach cures everything»: There is no evidence for this.

Common uses

  • Heart and artery health as part of a balanced diet
  • Moisturising skin and hair
  • A mild laxative

Usual preparation

Extra-virgin oil on food without heavy cooking, or rubbed on the skin.

Cautions

  • High in calories
  • Concentrated olive-leaf extract may lower blood pressure and blood sugar

Possible drug interactions

  • Leaf extract with blood-pressure and diabetes medicines
