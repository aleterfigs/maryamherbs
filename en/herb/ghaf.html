Ghaf

Prosopis cineraria
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
الغافة، الحنبل، الهنبل · Ghaf tree
Part used
Leaves and pods (hanbal)
Strength of evidence
Limited
For children
Not given to children.
DiarrhoeaEasy to grow at home

Common uses

  • Folk use in the UAE: the leaves for diarrhoea, as elderly women recall
  • Its pods (hanbal) feed livestock and were eaten in hard times
  • In India and Pakistan: the leaves for mouth ulcers and wounds, the bark for diarrhoea and asthma

Usual preparation

There is no reliable preparation or known dose; it is listed here for heritage, not for use.

Cautions

  • There are no human trials at all; studies are only in the lab and in animals
  • A tree protected by law in the UAE: do not cut it, strip its bark or break its branches
  • Giving its flowers in pregnancy, as in Indian medicine, has no evidence and should not be followed

Possible drug interactions

  • Unknown, as there are no studies
