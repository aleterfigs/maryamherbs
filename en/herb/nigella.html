Extended guide

Its forms
  • Whole seeds: lightly toasted and ground when used; they keep longer.
  • Powder: loses its aromatic oil quickly, so grind small amounts.
  • Oil: cold-pressed, with a sharp smell and pungent taste; the form most used in studies.
  • Capsules: oil in a fixed dose; the most accurate amount.
In the Sunnah and scholars' understanding

The Prophet ﷺ said: “In the black seed is healing for every disease except death” (Bukhari and Muslim).

Many commentators on the hadith held that «every disease» is a general phrase with a specific meaning — that it benefits many illnesses that can be treated with it, not that it replaces every treatment. Treating with it does not conflict with using medicine, for the Prophet ﷺ said: “Seek treatment, servants of God.”

What does the science say?
  • Moderate A slight drop in mildly raised blood pressure.
  • Moderate A slight drop in bad cholesterol and triglycerides.
  • Moderate A slight improvement in blood sugar in type 2 diabetes, alongside medicine, not instead of it.
  • Limited Nasal allergy and mild asthma.
  • Limited A helper in treating stomach H. pylori alongside medical treatment.
  • Not true Treating cancer: the studies are lab-only, with no evidence in humans.

Recent systematic reviews, including one in 2026, found modest improvements in lipids, blood sugar and blood pressure in people with diabetes and obesity, and said larger, more rigorous trials are needed.

Amounts used in studies
  • Ground seeds: 1–3 grams daily (about half to one teaspoon).
  • Oil: 500 mg to 1 g capsules twice daily.
  • Usually for 8–12 weeks, then assess the benefit.
Safety
  • Its oil on the skin may cause allergic inflammation and blisters: test on a small area first.
  • It may lower blood sugar and blood pressure, so monitor readings with their medicines.
  • It may slightly increase bleeding: caution with blood thinners, and stop two weeks before surgery.
  • Rare cases of kidney damage with large doses have been recorded: do not exceed the usual amounts.
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women: food amounts only.
Buying and storage
  • Good oil is cold-pressed, in a dark bottle, with a strong, sharp smell.
  • Keep away from light and heat, and use within months of opening.
  • Good seeds are glossy black and release their scent when rubbed.
Names and look-alikes
  • Names: Habbat al-barakah, the black seed, shuniz in heritage books, and «kalonji» in India and Pakistan.
  • Not black cumin: Its oil is sold as «black cumin oil», but true black cumin is a different plant (Bunium persicum).
  • Not black sesame: Black sesame seeds are flat and odourless, while black seed is small, triangular and smells sharp when rubbed.
  • «Onion seeds»: A wrong name it is sometimes sold under in Europe.
History
  • Ancient Egypt: Its seeds were found in the tomb of King Tutankhamun.
  • The Greeks: Dioscorides mentioned it in «De Materia Medica» for colds and headache.
  • Arab medicine: Ibn Sina and Ibn al-Baytar mentioned it as shuniz for colds, phlegm and the stomach.
  • In the Sunnah: The most famous item of prophetic medicine, with an agreed-upon hadith.
Black seed oil: how to tell good from fake?

The most important active substance in the oil is thymoquinone, and its amount varies greatly between products. In a study of oils sold in Malaysia, the strongest had about 27 times more than the weakest, and a 2022 Swiss study found an even bigger difference. So you may buy an oil that hardly contains the studied substance.

Commonest frauds and weaknesses:

  • Mixing it with cheap oils such as sunflower or sesame.
  • Extracting it with heat or solvents, which weakens its compounds.
  • Old or poorly stored seeds, or oil past its expiry date.

What helps:

  • Smell and taste: Good oil has a strong, sharp smell and a bitter, pungent taste that catches the throat. Mild-tasting oil without smell is suspect.
  • Colour: From amber to dark brown, and unfiltered oil may have a natural sediment at the bottom.
  • The label: «100% cold-pressed», the country of origin and the production date. The best companies state the thymoquinone percentage.
  • The container: Dark glass.
  • Price: Good virgin oil is not cheap.
  • Do not rely on the fridge or burning test: They do not detect fraud; only laboratory analysis is conclusive.
On skin and hair
  • Important warning: Severe allergic reactions have been reported from applying black seed oil to the skin, some with widespread blisters needing hospital care, published in dermatology journals.
  • Dilute it with another oil such as olive oil, and test it first on a small area for two days.
  • Stop immediately if itching, redness or blisters appear, and see a doctor if the rash spreads.
  • Do not put it on a baby's skin or near the eyes.
  • Hair loss: Studies on it are few and small.
Common myths
  • «It cures cancer and every disease»: There is no evidence in humans, and scholars understood the hadith «healing for every disease» as benefit for many diseases, not abandoning treatment.
  • «The more you take, the better»: Cases of kidney harm have been reported with large doses, and it may lower blood sugar and pressure too much with medicines.
  • «Its oil is safe on the skin because it is natural»: It is one of the natural oils that most often cause severe skin allergy.
  • «The whole seed is useless»: It is useful if chewed or ground; swallowed whole, it may pass through unchanged.
Growing it in the UAE
  • A winter crop: Sown in November and December and harvested in spring after about 4 months, before the heat sets in.
  • Sowing: Sow directly in the ground 1 to 2 cm deep, as it dislikes transplanting. It germinates within 10 to 15 days.
  • Soil and water: Light, well-drained soil and full sun, with moderate watering that is reduced as the seeds ripen.
  • Harvest: When the pods dry and turn yellow, before they split and drop the seeds; then dry them in the shade and beat them to release the seeds.
  • For the farmer: Its pretty flowers attract bees, which benefits nearby apiaries.

Common uses

  • Support for blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol (slight drops in small trials)
  • Nasal allergy and mild asthma symptoms (limited evidence)
  • An everyday addition to bread and food

Usual preparation

The seeds are eaten roasted and ground with honey, or sprinkled on food.

Cautions

  • The oil on the skin may cause allergic dermatitis
  • Pregnancy: food amounts only

Possible drug interactions

  • Diabetes and blood-pressure medicines (added effect)
  • Blood thinners
  • Sedatives
