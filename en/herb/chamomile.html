Chamomile

Matricaria chamomilla
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
البابونق، الكاموميل · Chamomile
Part used
Flowers
Strength of evidence
Moderate
For children
From age one, in a diluted amount (half a cup or less), after asking the paediatrician.
Sleep & anxietyDigestion & bloatingSkin & woundsDiarrhoeaEasy to grow at home

Common uses

  • Better sleep quality and mild anxiety
  • Soothing indigestion and colic
  • Compresses for mild skin irritation

Usual preparation

Infusion: one teaspoon of dried flowers in a cup of boiling water, covered for 5–10 minutes.

Cautions

  • May cause allergy in people sensitive to the daisy family (chrysanthemum, ragweed)
  • Ask a doctor during pregnancy

Possible drug interactions

  • Blood thinners such as warfarin
  • Sedatives and sleeping pills (may increase drowsiness)

Growing and propagation

Easy in the Gulf
Origin and climate
An annual from the Mediterranean and Europe that likes a mild, cool climate
In the Gulf
Sown in winter (October–November) and flowers in spring; it cannot take summer heat
Light, water and soil
Full sun in winter, moderate watering, light well-drained soil
Propagation
By seed: scatter on the soil surface without covering (it needs light to germinate); germinates in 1–2 weeks
Harvest
Pick the fully open flowers in the morning and dry them in the shade
