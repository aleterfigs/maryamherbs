Desert truffle (faqaa)

Terfezia / Tirmania spp.
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
الفقع، الكمأ، الترفاس، الزبيدي، الجمّة · Desert truffle
Part used
Underground fruiting body
Strength of evidence
Limited
For children
Only well cooked in food.
Fatigue & recovery

In Islamic heritage

Authentic (sahih) hadithThe Prophet ﷺ said: “Truffles are a kind of manna, and their water is a cure for the eye” (Bukhari and Muslim). Scholars differed: some said its water is mixed into medicines, others that it is used alone, and the matter is referred to physicians.

Common uses

  • A nourishing food in season (protein and minerals)
  • Lab studies suggest antioxidant and antimicrobial properties, without enough human trials

Usual preparation

Wash well to remove sand, peel, and cook with onion or rice, or grill.

Cautions

  • Never drip its juice into the eye: it can carry serious infections. Many scholars understood the hadith to mean it goes into eye medicines prepared by experts
  • Cook well and buy from a trusted source, as it can be mixed up with poisonous fungi
  • People allergic to mushrooms may react to it

Possible drug interactions

  • No known interactions in food amounts

Sources

  • The hadith text in Sahih al-Bukhari (Sunnah.com) — in Arabic and English
  • List of official monographs of the European Medicines Agency (EMA) — search it by the Latin name: Terfezia

If symptoms last more than a week or get worse, see a doctor. Always tell your doctor or pharmacist about any herbs you take.
