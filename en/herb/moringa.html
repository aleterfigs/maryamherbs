Which part is safe?
  • Leaves: the safest; eaten as a vegetable in India, Africa and the Philippines.
  • Green pods: cooked in Indian cuisine (sambar).
  • Seeds and their oil: ban oil for skin, hair and food.
  • Roots and bark: they contain toxic substances and may cause the womb to contract; do not use them.
What does the science say?
  • Relatively strong The leaves are a food rich in protein, calcium, iron and vitamin A, useful for nutrition where food is scarce.
  • Limited Lowering blood sugar and lipids: small studies with inconsistent results.
  • Limited Increasing breast milk: small encouraging trials.
  • Limited Anaemia: a plant source of iron, but it does not replace a doctor's treatment.
  • The claim that it «treats 300 diseases» has no scientific basis.
Usual amount

One to two teaspoons of leaf powder daily in food or warm water. Start with half a teaspoon, as it may cause diarrhoea or an upset stomach at first.

Before buying
  • Buy from a licensed source; contaminants and heavy metals have been found in some powders.
  • A bright green colour and a grassy smell are signs of a good powder.
  • Genuine ban oil is rare and expensive, and often adulterated.

Common uses

  • A leaf food supplement (protein, calcium, iron and vitamin A)
  • Possible support for blood sugar and lipids (small studies)
  • Increasing breast milk (small trials)
  • Ban oil for skin and hair

Usual preparation

Half a teaspoon of leaf powder in warm water or food.

Cautions

  • The root and bark are forbidden in pregnancy (may cause contractions); the leaves only after asking the doctor
  • May lower blood sugar and blood pressure
  • May upset the stomach in large amounts
  • Safety data in breastfeeding are insufficient, and some references advise avoiding it

Possible drug interactions

  • Diabetes and blood-pressure medicines
  • Thyroid medicine (levothyroxine)

Growing and propagation

Easy in the Gulf
Origin and climate
A fast tree from India, Africa and Arabia
In the Gulf
One of the best trees for the Gulf: it loves heat and grows two metres in its first year
Light, water and soil
Full sun, moderate watering, sandy well-drained soil (standing water kills it)
Propagation
By seed (germinates within a week) or large, thick cuttings
Harvest
The leaves at any time; cut the tips to keep it short and bushy, and dry the leaves in the shade
🌡️ Ideal temperaturefrom 25 to 38 °CStruggles above 48°C · harmed below 10°C
