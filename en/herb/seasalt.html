Salt water (sea salt)

Sodium chloride (saline)
See real photos ↗
Other names
الماء والملح، المحلول الملحي، ملح البحر، غرغرة · Saline, sea salt
Part used
Salt dissolved in water
Strength of evidence
Moderate
For children
Gargling from age 6, once the child can spit it out; for the nose, a ready-made saline spray from the pharmacy suits all ages.
From the seaColds & coughMouth & throatSeasonal allergies

In Islamic heritage

Authentic (sahih) hadithThe Prophet ﷺ was asked about making ablution with sea water and said: “Its water is pure and its dead are lawful” (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i and Ibn Majah; Tirmidhi said: good and authentic).

Common uses

  • Gargle for a sore throat
  • Nasal rinse for colds, sinusitis and allergy
  • Mouth rinse after a tooth extraction (after the first day and with the dentist's permission)

Usual preparation

To gargle: a quarter to half a teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water. For the nose: a ready-made pharmacy solution, or boiled then cooled water with salt (see the recipe).

Cautions

  • Rinse the nose only with boiled-then-cooled or distilled water, never tap water: rare, fatal brain infections have been recorded from unsterilised tap water
  • Do not swallow the gargle water
  • People with high blood pressure should not drink salt water

Possible drug interactions

  • No interactions for external use
