Turmeric

Curcuma longa
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
العقدة الصفراء، الهرد · Turmeric
Part used
Root (rhizome)
Strength of evidence
Moderate
For children
Not recommended for children except as prescribed by a doctor.
Pain & inflammationDigestion & bloating

Common uses

  • Osteoarthritis pain
  • Indigestion
  • As an everyday spice in food

Usual preparation

In food or as "golden milk": one teaspoon of turmeric with a pinch of black pepper in warm milk.

Cautions

  • Concentrated supplements have been linked to rare cases of liver injury
  • Avoid with gallstones
  • Pregnancy: as a spice only

Possible drug interactions

  • Blood thinners
  • Diabetes medicines
  • Some chemotherapy drugs

Growing and propagation

Possible with care
Origin and climate
A tropical rhizome from India that likes heat and humidity
In the Gulf
Planted in spring in a large pot in part shade; it rests in winter
Light, water and soil
Part shade, regular watering, rich well-drained soil
Propagation
With a fresh rhizome from the shop: a piece with two buds, 5 cm deep
Harvest
After 8–10 months when the leaves wilt; the rhizome is boiled, then dried and ground
🌡️ Ideal temperaturefrom 22 to 32 °CStruggles above 40°C · harmed below 12°C
🧂 Tolerance of salty waterLowFresh or desalinated water (under about 1,000 ppm). Salty well water burns the leaf tips.
🧪 Soil acidity (pH)from 5.5 to 7Gulf soil is alkaline (7.5–8.5): mix in organic compost and a little agricultural sulphur, or grow it in a pot with ready-made potting mix.
💧 WateringWinter: Every 4–5 days (it rests, so water less)Summer: Every two days
Life cycle
Perennial (rhizome)
Where to grow
A 35–40 cm pot
First harvest
8–10 months
Common pests
Rhizome rot, scale insects
