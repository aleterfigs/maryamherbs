Cinnamon

Cinnamomum verum / cassia
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
الدارسين، الدارصيني · Cinnamon
Part used
Bark
Strength of evidence
Limited
For children
As a spice in food only.
Blood sugar & lipidsDigestion & bloating

Common uses

  • Possible support for blood sugar
  • Wind and indigestion
  • Flavouring for drinks and food

Usual preparation

A cinnamon stick in a cup of water, simmered gently for 10 minutes.

Cautions

  • Cassia cinnamon (the most common) is rich in coumarin, which harms the liver in large amounts; Ceylon cinnamon is safer
  • May irritate the mouth in some people

Possible drug interactions

  • Diabetes medicines (risk of low blood sugar)
  • Medicines that affect the liver

Growing and propagation

Not usually grown in the Gulf; bought
Origin and climate
A tropical tree from Sri Lanka and India
In the Gulf
Not usually grown at home; it needs high humidity and a tropical climate
Propagation
By seed or cuttings in tropical regions
Harvest
The bark of young branches is peeled and left to curl and dry
