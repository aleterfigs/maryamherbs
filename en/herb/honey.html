There is not enough evidence to prefer one kind over another for home use, except sterile medical honey for treating wounds.

What does the science say?
  • Relatively strong Night-time cough in children over one and adults; some British medical guidelines recommend trying it for acute cough.
  • Moderate Superficial wounds and burns with sterile medical honey (not kitchen honey).
  • Moderate Mouth inflammation from radiotherapy, after asking the treating team.
  • Limited Small mouth ulcers and an irritated throat.
  • Not true Weight loss and treating diabetes: honey is a natural sugar that raises blood sugar.
Common myths
  • «Crystallised honey is fake»: not true; crystallisation is natural and happens faster in clover honey and in the cold. Warm it in a water bath to make it liquid again.
  • «The thumb, water or lighter test reveals fake honey»: these tests are unreliable; laboratory testing is what decides.
  • «Heating honey turns it into poison»: not true; strong, long heating loses some enzymes and flavour, but adding it to a warm drink is safe. It is best added once the drink is lukewarm.
  • «Honey never goes off»: good honey keeps a very long time, but moisture makes it ferment.
Storage
  • In a tightly closed glass jar at room temperature, away from the sun.
  • Do not keep it in the fridge, as cold speeds up crystallisation.
  • Always use a clean, dry spoon so no moisture gets in.
Important warnings
  • Completely forbidden under one year of age in any form, even on a dummy, because of the risk of botulism.
  • Diabetics count it as part of their sugars.
  • «Mad honey» from rhododendron flowers in Turkey and Nepal is poisonous and causes a fall in blood pressure and pulse.
  • People allergic to bees or pollen should be careful with raw honey and propolis.
  • Kitchen honey must not be put on open wounds; only sterile medical honey.

Common uses

  • Soothing night-time cough (from age one)
  • Soothing the throat
  • Sterile medical-grade honey is used in hospitals for wounds and burns

Usual preparation

One teaspoon in warm (not boiling) water, or with herbs once they have cooled.

Cautions

  • Completely forbidden for babies under one (risk of botulism)
  • High in sugar: counts toward a diabetic's sugar allowance
  • Do not put kitchen honey on open wounds; only sterile medical honey

Possible drug interactions

  • Diabetes medicines (raises blood sugar)
