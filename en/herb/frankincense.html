«Kundur» is its name in old medical books, and «male frankincense» means the good round tears. In Egypt, «luban» means chewing gum.

History
  • The incense route: Caravans carried it from Dhofar and Hadramawt north across Arabia to Gaza and the Mediterranean ports, enriching the kingdoms of Saba, Hadramawt, Ma'in and Qataban, and later the Nabataeans.
  • «The Land of Frankincense»: UNESCO inscribed it as World Heritage in 2000. It includes the trees of Wadi Dawkah, the oasis of Shisr (Wubar), and the ports of Khor Rori (Sumhuram) and Al-Baleed in Dhofar.
  • Ancient Egypt: Queen Hatshepsut sent an expedition to the land of «Punt» that returned with frankincense trees, and the journey was carved on the walls of her temple at Deir el-Bahari.
  • Arab medicine: Ibn Sina mentioned it as kundur for cough and the stomach, and it was famous among them for strengthening memory.
  • In the hadith: No specific authentic hadith exists about frankincense. Ibn al-Qayyim said the hadith «Fumigate your homes with frankincense and wormwood» is not authentic, and the story circulated about burning frankincense in Madinah during a plague has no basis.
Gulf customs
  • Chewing: A small tear chewed like gum to freshen the mouth.
  • Frankincense water: Tears soaked overnight, and the water drunk for the stomach.
  • Fumigation: In homes, majlis gatherings, at Eid and when welcoming guests.
  • Fumigating water jars: An old Omani custom of perfuming the clay jar with smoke before filling it, to sweeten the taste of the water.
What does the science say?
  • Moderate Knee osteoarthritis: A 2020 review of 7 trials with 545 patients found that boswellia extract eased pain and stiffness and improved movement, when taken for at least 4 weeks. Most of these trials used standardised extracts of the Indian type, not soaked frankincense.
  • Limited Inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis and Crohn's): Small, conflicting trials, and a larger trial in Crohn's disease found no difference from placebo.
  • Limited Asthma: Few small trials.
  • Limited Memory: Despite its fame in heritage, most studies are in animals.
  • Limited Cancer: Laboratory results only, and frankincense is not a cancer treatment.
  • Soaked frankincense water has hardly been studied, and the active substances in frankincense dissolve poorly in water.
Incense and the lungs

In a 2009 Omani study of 2441 children, burning incense more than twice a week was linked to children's breathing being affected, and incense triggered wheezing in about 38% of children with asthma. It was not linked to more children developing asthma itself.

  • Burn incense in an open space or with good ventilation.
  • No incense in the room of a baby or of someone with asthma or allergy.
  • Burning charcoal releases carbon monoxide, so do not leave it lit in a closed room where people sleep.
Amount and use
  • Frankincense water: 5 to 6 small tears soaked in a cup of water overnight.
  • Chewing: One small pure tear, not swallowed.
  • Joint supplements: A standardised extract from a trusted source at the dose on the package; in studies, 100 mg to 1 gram a day depending on the type of extract.
Safety
  • It may cause stomach upset or heartburn.
  • The essential oil is not to be swallowed, and is diluted before putting it on the skin, as it may cause allergy.
  • With medicines: Consult a doctor if you take blood thinners or anti-inflammatory painkillers.
  • Pregnancy: Avoid extracts. Frankincense water is common but its safety in pregnancy has not been studied, so keep to moderation and ask your doctor.
  • Children: Do not give frankincense tears to young children because of the risk of choking.
Common myths
  • «Frankincense water in pregnancy makes the baby intelligent»: There is no evidence for this, and its safety in pregnancy has not been studied.
  • «Frankincense oil cures cancer»: Laboratory results only, and leaving treatment for it is dangerous.
  • «Frankincense strengthens students' memory»: An old tradition, and the evidence in humans is weak.
  • «Incense purifies the air at home»: It adds smoke and fine particles; it does not purify the air.
Buying and quality
  • Good Hojari: Large, semi-transparent, greenish-white tears with a lemony, piney scent, which turn gum-like when chewed.
  • Small, broken, dusty tears are cheaper and suit burning.
  • Beware of dyed tears or tears mixed with cheap gums.
  • Keep it in an airtight container away from heat.
  • «Ma'moul» and scented «bakhoor» mixtures are not pure frankincense.
Growing it in the UAE
  • Will it grow? Possible but difficult. It tolerates the heat and dryness of the UAE, but coastal humidity and heavy watering rot its roots. In Dhofar it grows on rocky limestone slopes, and its finest Hojari frankincense comes from dry areas far from the humidity of the monsoon season.
  • Soil: Rocky, gravelly and very fast-draining. In the garden, plant it on a mound or a heap of gravel; in a pot, use a mix of sand, gravel and a little soil, like a cactus mix.
  • Propagation: Large cuttings are easier: a branch as thick as a thumb or more, its cut left to dry for a few days, then planted in dry sand and watered very little until it roots. Seeds germinate poorly and slowly.
  • Watering: Every 2 to 3 weeks in winter and every week in summer, letting the soil dry out completely between waterings. It drops its leaves in drought or cold, which is normal, so do not add water then.
  • Slow growth: It grows slowly, and its bark is not cut until many years later, when its trunk has thickened.
  • Tapping in moderation: In Dhofar, trees are tapped in the hot dry months and then left to rest. Overtapping weakens the tree and reduces its seeds.
  • A threatened tree: The International Union for Conservation of Nature lists Dhofar frankincense as «Near Threatened». In Africa, Ethiopian frankincense trees are declining because of overtapping, grazing, fires and beetles, and researchers have predicted that production could halve within 20 years.
  • Seedlings: You may find them in Dhofar nurseries, and moving plants between countries is subject to agricultural quarantine.

Common uses

  • Osteoarthritis pain (standardised extracts)
  • Chewed for gum health (traditional use)
  • Frankincense-soaked water for the stomach (traditional use)

Usual preparation

Soak a few pieces of frankincense in a cup of water overnight and drink the water.

Cautions

  • May cause an upset stomach or heartburn
  • Incense: repeated inhalation in a closed room irritates the lungs
  • Pregnancy: ask the doctor

Possible drug interactions

  • Blood thinners
  • Anti-inflammatory medicines (added effect)
