Aloe vera (gel)

Aloe vera
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
الصبر، الألوفيرا · Aloe vera
Part used
Inner gel
Strength of evidence
Moderate
For children
The gel on the skin is suitable for small superficial burns. Not to be given by mouth.
Skin & woundsEasy to grow at home

Common uses

  • Sunburn and minor superficial burns
  • Moisturising dry skin

Usual preparation

Apply the clear gel directly to clean skin several times a day.

Cautions

  • External use is the safe one; the yellow latex sap is a strong laxative and can do harm
  • Not for deep wounds or large burns

Possible drug interactions

  • When drunk: diabetes medicines, diuretics, digoxin

Growing and propagation

Easy in the Gulf
Origin and climate
A succulent from the Arabian Peninsula and Africa
In the Gulf
One of the easiest plants in the Gulf all year round; it tolerates heat and drought
Light, water and soil
Sun or light shade, very little water (every two weeks), sandy well-drained soil
Propagation
By offsets: the small plants around the mother are separated with their roots and planted
Harvest
Cut a large lower leaf at its base and stand it upright so the yellow latex drains before taking the gel
🌡️ Ideal temperaturefrom 18 to 35 °CStruggles above 45°C · harmed below 5°C
🧂 Tolerance of salty waterModerateTolerates water up to about 2,000–3,000 ppm, with the soil flushed with fresh water now and then.
🧪 Soil acidity (pH)from 6 to 8Suits the Gulf's alkaline soil.
💧 WateringWinter: Every 2–3 weeksSummer: Every 10–14 days
Life cycle
Perennial succulent
Where to grow
A 25 cm pot with cactus soil
First harvest
One to two years for large leaves
Common pests
Root rot from overwatering, mealybug
