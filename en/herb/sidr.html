Extended guide

Parts of the sidr tree
  • Leaves: dried and ground; they contain foaming substances that clean like soap.
  • Nabq: a small sweet-sour fruit, eaten fresh or dried.
  • Sidr honey: one of the most famous honeys of Yemen and Saudi Arabia.
  • The tree: the shade of the desert; its roots withstand drought.
How to prepare a sidr wash
  1. Mix 3 tablespoons of ground sidr leaves with warm water into a paste.
  2. Whisk by hand or with a spoon until it foams.
  3. Spread it on the hair or body and leave for 10–15 minutes, then rinse well.
What does the science say?
  • Limited Lab studies suggest antifungal and antibacterial properties in sidr leaves.
  • There are not enough human trials for treating dandruff or hair loss, but it is a gentle cleanser that is safe for most people.
  • Nabq is a food rich in vitamin C.
Safety
  • It may dry dry hair, so follow with oil.
  • Keep away from the eyes.
  • Test on a small area if your skin is sensitive.
  • The nabq stone is hard: remove it for children to avoid choking.

Common uses

  • A natural wash for hair and scalp
  • Easing dandruff (traditional use)
  • Washing the body
  • Nabq: a fruit rich in vitamin C

Usual preparation

Mix ground leaves with warm water until it foams, and wash with it.

Cautions

  • May dry dry hair: follow with oil
  • Test on a small area
  • Not to be drunk during pregnancy or breastfeeding

Possible drug interactions

  • No known interactions for external use
