Stages of ripening
  • Tal': the first growth from the palm.
  • Balah or khalal: green then yellow or red, with an astringent taste.
  • Busr: full colour, sweetness beginning.
  • Rutab: soft and juicy, with more water and less sugar per date.
  • Tamr: dried, with concentrated sugar; keeps for a long time.
In the Quran and Sunnah
  • God said to Maryam during labour: “Shake the trunk of the palm tree toward you; it will drop on you ripe, fresh dates” [Quran 19:25].
  • «Whoever eats seven ajwa dates every morning will not be harmed that day by poison or magic” (Bukhari and Muslim).
  • «A house without dates — its people are hungry” (Muslim).
  • The Prophet ﷺ used to break his fast with fresh dates; if there were none, then dried dates; if there were none, he took a few sips of water (Abu Dawud and Tirmidhi).
  • The Prophet ﷺ used to do tahnik for newborns with a date (Bukhari and Muslim).
What does the science say?
  • Relatively strong A food rich in fibre, potassium, magnesium and antioxidants.
  • Moderate The last weeks of pregnancy: trials and reviews found that eating about 6 dates a day in the last four weeks was linked to better cervical readiness and less need for induction. Ask your doctor, especially with gestational diabetes.
  • Moderate Constipation, thanks to the fibre.
  • Moderate Despite their sweetness, many varieties have a moderate glycaemic index, but the amount matters most for diabetics.
The Sunnah of tahnik and the newborn's safety

Tahnik is chewing a little date and rubbing it on the newborn's palate.

  • Wash the hands well, chew the date until very soft, and rub it on the palate with a very light touch.
  • Very important: no one with a cold sore (herpes) on the lip or a cold should do tahnik; serious herpes infections in newborns caught from adults' saliva have been recorded.
  • Do not put pieces of date in a newborn's mouth, and do not give date water or honey to a baby.
For diabetes and kidney patients
  • Diabetes: two or three dates within the allowance, with protein or healthy fats (yoghurt, nuts) to soften the rise in blood sugar, and measure your sugar afterwards to learn their effect on you.
  • Advanced kidney disease: dates are rich in potassium, so agree the amount with the doctor.
Storage and buying
  • Fresh dates (rutab) are kept in the fridge and eaten within days; they can be frozen.
  • Dried dates in an airtight container in a cool, dry place, or in the fridge for longer.
  • Avoid dates coated with glucose or shiny syrup.
  • Date stones are roasted and ground into a caffeine-free «coffee»; data on its benefits are few.

Common uses

  • Quick energy when breaking a fast and when tired
  • Constipation, thanks to the fibre
  • The last weeks of pregnancy (moderate evidence)
  • A traditional food after childbirth

Usual preparation

Eaten as they are, or soaked in water or milk.

Cautions

  • High in sugar: for diabetics, two or three dates within the allowance
  • High in potassium: caution in kidney disease
  • Remove the stone and cut up for young children

Possible drug interactions

  • No important drug interactions; kidney patients on potassium-sparing medicines should ask their doctor
