Rosemary

Salvia rosmarinus
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
حصى لبان، أزير، الروزماري · Rosemary
Part used
Leaves
Strength of evidence
Limited
For children
Not recommended for children except as prescribed by a doctor.
Digestion & bloatingPain & inflammationSkin & woundsHair & scalp

Common uses

  • Indigestion
  • Diluted massage for muscle pain
  • Diluted oil for the scalp (a small study on hair loss)

Usual preparation

Infusion: one teaspoon in a cup of boiling water for 10 minutes.

Cautions

  • Medicinal amounts are avoided in pregnancy
  • Avoid the concentrated oil with epilepsy

Possible drug interactions

  • Blood thinners
  • Diuretics
  • Lithium

Growing and propagation

Possible with care
Origin and climate
A perennial Mediterranean shrub that tolerates drought
In the Gulf
Planted in autumn; needs part shade and dry soil in summer
Light, water and soil
Full sun, little water, sandy well-drained soil
Propagation
By cuttings: a 10 cm sprig with the lower leaves removed, pushed into moist sandy soil
Harvest
Cut the tips of the stems at any time
