In Islamic heritage

Myrtle appears in Andalusian books of simples such as Ibn al-Baytar and al-Ghafiqi, and it is still placed on graves and used in the Maghreb.

Common uses

  • Mouthwash for the gums
  • A rinse to strengthen hair (traditionally)
  • Reducing sweating (traditionally)

Usual preparation

Boil a handful of leaves in two cups of water for 10 minutes, for external use.

Cautions

  • Myrtle essential oil must not be swallowed and may cause breathing spasm in children
  • In moderation in pregnancy
  • Mostly for external use

Possible drug interactions

  • No interactions for external use
