Ithmid (kohl)

Stibnite (Sb₂S₃)
See real photos of the plant ↗
Other names
الكحل، الكحل العربي · Kohl, antimony
Part used
Ground mineral stone
Strength of evidence
Unsafe
For children
Not recommended for children except as prescribed by a doctor.

In Islamic heritage

Good (hasan) hadithThe Prophet ﷺ said: “The best of your kohl is ithmid: it clears the sight and makes the lashes grow” (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah; graded good by Tirmidhi). True ithmid is antimony stone, but much of what is sold in markets is lead galena.

Common uses

  • Adornment and following the Sunnah with a licensed kohl that lists its ingredients

Usual preparation

Use a kohl or eyeliner licensed by a regulator, with a clean applicator or pencil for each person.

Cautions

  • Most unlicensed traditional kohl contains lead, which is absorbed through the eyes and hands
  • Never apply it to babies and children, and do not use kohl of unknown origin in pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • Do not share the applicator, to avoid eye infections

Possible drug interactions

  • No drug interactions; the danger is lead poisoning
