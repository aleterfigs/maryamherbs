Foot and nail fungus

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

The basics are drying the feet after wudu and bathing, cotton socks and airing shoes. Diabetics should not treat their feet at home without a doctor.
Why it is common in the Gulf
  • Heat, sweat and closed shoes all day.
  • Wudu: feet wet five times a day without drying between the toes are an ideal environment for fungus. Dry between your toes after every wudu.
  • The floors of mosques, public bathrooms, swimming pools and gyms.
What really helps
  • Relatively strong Drying well especially between the toes, with your own towel.
  • Relatively strong Cotton socks changed daily, airing shoes and alternating them every other day, and open sandals at home.
  • Moderate Slippers in public bathrooms and pools, and do not share towels, shoes or nail clippers.
  • Continue care for two weeks after the itching goes, as the fungus comes back if you stop early.
Herbs and fungus
  • Moderate Tea tree oil: in one trial, a 25–50% solution cured athlete's foot more often than placebo, but it causes skin allergy in some people. We use it diluted to 5%, and it must never be swallowed.
  • Limited Soaking the foot in diluted vinegar (a cup of vinegar in a bowl of water): traditional and drying, with little evidence, and not for cracked skin.
  • Nail fungus: a nail takes 12–18 months to renew, and oils rarely cure it alone. Another condition such as psoriasis can look similar, so it needs a diagnosis.
Myths about fungus
  • «Soaking the foot in bleach, kerosene or petrol»: it burns the skin and causes a worse infection.
  • «Fungus comes from poor hygiene»: it can affect the cleanest people; the cause is moisture and heat.
  • «The itching has gone, so it is cured»: the fungus stays for weeks after the symptoms go.

Diluted tea tree oil for foot fungus

An antiseptic oil studied for athlete's foot, diluted in olive oil so it does not irritate the skin.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Tea tree oil (Moderate).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Twice a day, for 4 weeks.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Tea tree oil: 15 One drop of tea tree oil
  • Olives and olive oil: 1 tablespoon olive oil (15 ml)
Method
  1. Mix the two oils in a small dark bottle.
  2. Test a drop on your forearm first and wait a day: if the skin turns red or itchy, do not use it.
  3. Wash the foot and dry it well, especially between the toes.
  4. Apply to the affected area with cotton wool twice a day.
  5. Continue for two weeks after the itching stops.
Note

For external use only. It is poisonous if swallowed and must be kept away from children. Do not apply to an open wound. Diabetics should see a doctor first.

Qaraz decoction for the feet

An astringent wash from Arabian heritage for sweaty, smelly feet.

Limited evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Qaraz (Limited).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once or twice a day when needed.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Qaraz (acacia pods): A handful of crushed qaraz pods
  • A litre of water
Method
  1. Boil the qaraz in the water for 15 minutes.
  2. Strain and add to a basin of lukewarm water.
  3. Soak the feet for 15 minutes, then dry them well.
Note

External use only. May stain the skin slightly.

Thyme foot soak

To refresh the feet and reduce odour.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once a day or every other day.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Thyme: 3 tablespoons of thyme
  • A litre of hot water
  • A spoon of salt
Method
  1. Steep the thyme in the water for 10 minutes.
  2. Pour into a basin with lukewarm water until the temperature is comfortable.
  3. Soak the feet for 15 minutes, then dry them well.
Note

People with diabetes should check the water temperature with their hand to avoid burns.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • A diabetic with a wound, redness or swelling of the foot: see a doctor immediately, as diabetic foot infection is dangerous
  • Redness spreading up the leg, fever or pus
  • Nail fungus rarely clears with home treatment and needs a diagnosis
  • No improvement after two weeks of care
