Sinusitis

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

Most sinusitis is viral and heals within two to three weeks. The most useful thing at home is rinsing the nose with salt water, using boiled and cooled water only.
Most sinusitis heals by itself
  • Most of it follows a cold and is caused by viruses; it improves within 2–3 weeks, and most cases do not need antibiotics.
  • Green or yellow mucus does not mean bacteria necessarily; it is just a sign of inflammation.
  • In the Gulf: dust and sandstorms, dry air-conditioning, and incense and strong perfumes irritate the nose and sinuses.
What really helps
  • Relatively strong Salt-water nasal rinse once or twice a day. Only with boiled then cooled water or distilled water, never straight from the tap, as rare but fatal brain infections have been recorded from tap water in the nose.
  • Moderate Rest and warm fluids, and raising the head slightly when sleeping.
  • Moderate Honey for the accompanying cough (for those over one year).
  • Limited Warm steam may ease the blocked feeling, but does not speed recovery. Do not give it to children from a bowl of boiling water because of the risk of burns.
  • A warm compress on the face eases the pain.
Herbs and the sinuses
  • Limited Eucalyptus and menthol inhaled: they give a feeling of openness. The concentrated oil is poisonous if swallowed and should not be used near the faces of young children.
  • Moderate A registered European blend of five herbs (gentian root, primula flower, elderflower, sorrel and verbena) has trials in sinusitis; it is bought as a pharmacy product, not made at home.
  • Ginger, lemon and honey make a soothing drink, without evidence of treating the sinuses.

Saline nasal rinse

For a blocked nose with colds, sinusitis and seasonal allergy.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Salt water (Moderate).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once or twice a day when needed.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Salt water (sea salt): A quarter teaspoon of pure non-iodised salt
  • A pinch of sodium bicarbonate (optional; reduces stinging)
  • A cup of water boiled for 3 minutes then cooled to lukewarm, or distilled water
Method
  1. Boil the water for 3 minutes and let it cool to lukewarm, or use distilled water.
  2. Dissolve the salt and bicarbonate.
  3. Lean over the sink and tilt your head to one side, and pour the solution with a nasal pot or squeeze bottle into the upper nostril so it comes out of the other, breathing through your open mouth.
  4. Repeat on the other side, then blow the nose gently.
  5. Wash and dry the pot, and make a fresh solution every day.
Note

Do not use tap water without boiling it: it rarely carries an organism that causes a fatal brain infection. For children: a ready-made saline spray from the pharmacy is easier and safer.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Warm cold-relief drink

To soothe the throat and feel warm during a cold.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Ginger (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One cup 2–3 times a day until the symptoms go, for a week at most; if no better, see a doctor.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Ginger: 3–4 Slices of fresh ginger
  • Cinnamon: A small cinnamon stick (Ceylon preferred)
  • Juice of half a lemon
  • One teaspoon of honey
Method
  1. Simmer the ginger and cinnamon in one and a half cups of water over a low heat for 10 minutes.
  2. Strain and let it cool a little.
  3. Add the lemon and honey once it is warm, not boiling.
Note

No honey for children under one year. See a doctor if the fever lasts more than 3 days or comes with shortness of breath.

Eucalyptus steam

Inhalation to open a blocked nose.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Eucalyptus (Moderate).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? A 5–10-minute session, once or twice a day, for a few days only.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Eucalyptus: A handful of eucalyptus leaves or 3 drops of oil
  • A bowl of hot water
Method
  1. Put the leaves in hot (not boiling) water.
  2. Cover your head with a towel, about 30 cm away.
  3. Inhale for 5–10 minutes with your eyes closed.
Note

Risk of burns. Not for children or with asthma.

Salt-water nasal rinse

One of the most effective ways to relieve nasal allergy symptoms.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once or twice a day in the allergy or cold season.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • A cup of boiled-then-cooled water (or sterile water)
  • A quarter teaspoon of non-iodised salt
  • A pinch of bicarbonate
Method
  1. Dissolve the salt and bicarbonate in the lukewarm water.
  2. Use a nasal rinse bottle or a neti pot.
  3. Rinse each nostril with the head tilted, then blow the nose gently.
Note

Use only boiled or sterile water, never tap water straight from the tap.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Swelling or redness around the eye, or a change in vision or double vision: an emergency, go immediately
  • Severe headache with a stiff neck, confusion or unusual drowsiness: an emergency
  • A high fever above 39
  • Symptoms for more than 10 days without improvement, or improving then coming back worse
  • Symptoms that keep recurring or last more than 12 weeks, or a nosebleed from one side
