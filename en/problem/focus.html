Focus & memory

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

7 suitable blends

The Maghreb

Atay with mint (Moroccan tea)

The tea of hospitality in Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and Mauritania, drunk after meals.

Relatively strong evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Mint (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One to two cups a day; not in the evening.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For a small teapot)
  • Green tea (matcha and sencha): One teaspoon of green tea (gunpowder or Chinese)
  • Mint: A bunch of fresh mint (about 10 sprigs)
  • Sugar to taste (less is better)
  • Half a litre of boiling water
Method
  1. Rinse the tea with a little boiling water and discard it.
  2. Add the rest of the water and leave on a low heat for 3 minutes.
  3. Add the mint and sugar, and pour from a height to make foam.
Note

It usually contains caffeine and a lot of sugar. Some add wormwood (shiba) in winter; we do not recommend it as it contains toxic thujone.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Japan

Sencha green tea

Japan's everyday tea, made with water below boiling.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Green tea (Moderate).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One to two cups a day; not in the evening.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Green tea (matcha and sencha): One teaspoon of sencha
  • A cup of water at about 75 °C
Method
  1. Boil the water and let it cool for two minutes.
  2. Steep the tea for one and a half to two minutes only.
  3. Strain; the same leaves can be steeped a second time.
Note

Contains caffeine: not in the evening, and drink between meals, not with them.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Yemen

Yemeni qishr

Yemen's hospitality drink of coffee husks with ginger.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Ginger (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One to two cups a day; not in the evening.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For two cups)
  • Coffee husk (Yemeni qishr): One tablespoon of coffee husks
  • Ginger: Half a teaspoon of ground ginger
  • Cinnamon: A pinch of cinnamon
  • Two cups of water
Method
  1. Simmer the ingredients in the water over a low heat for 10 minutes.
  2. Strain and sweeten lightly if you like.
Note

Contains a little caffeine.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Al-Andalus

Hasa luban (rosemary) with honey

This is what the people of al-Andalus and the Maghreb called rosemary, and they drank it for a «cold stomach».

Moderate evidenceNeeds caution for some groupsClassical medical books
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Honey (Moderate).
  • Safety: Rosemary: not suitable for some situations (pregnancy, breastfeeding, illnesses or medicines). Use «Suggest a blend» to find out whether it suits you.
  • Source: Classical medical books.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One cup once or twice a day; can be continued 4–6 weeks, then a week's break.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Rosemary: A sprig of rosemary
  • Honey: One teaspoon of honey
  • A cup of boiling water
Method
  1. Steep the sprig for 7 minutes.
  2. Strain and add the honey.
Note

Avoid with epilepsy, and medicinal amounts in pregnancy.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

The Gulf

Arabic coffee with cardamom and saffron

The Gulf coffee of hospitality, lightly roasted.

Limited evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Cardamom (Limited).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? A few small cups a day, not in the evening.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For a small dallah)
  • Two tablespoons of lightly roasted Arabic coffee
  • Cardamom: One teaspoon of ground cardamom
  • Saffron: Saffron threads
  • 3 cups of water
Method
  1. Boil the water, then add the coffee and let it simmer gently for 10 minutes.
  2. Turn off the heat and add the cardamom and saffron.
  3. Let it settle for two minutes, then pour into the dallah.
Note

Contains caffeine. Served with dates.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

The Levant

Syrian mate

The drink of the mountains of Syria and Lebanon, originally from South America.

Limited evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Yerba mate (Limited).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One session a day, not in the evening.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Yerba mate: The cup two-thirds full of mate
  • Water at 70–80 °C
Method
  1. Fill the cup with mate and tilt it, then pour a little lukewarm water first.
  2. Insert the straw, then pour the hot (not boiling) water.
  3. Refill the water several times.
Note

High in caffeine. Do not drink it very hot.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Europe

Rosemary tea for focus

Small studies suggest its scent may slightly improve attention.

Traditional, no studiesNeeds caution for some groupsInherited folk custom

  • Safety: Rosemary: not suitable for some situations (pregnancy, breastfeeding, illnesses or medicines). Use «Suggest a blend» to find out whether it suits you.

How much and when? One cup once or twice a day; can be continued 4–6 weeks, then a week's break.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Rosemary: A fresh rosemary sprig
  • A cup of boiling water
Method
  1. Steep the sprig for 5–7 minutes.
  2. Drink in the morning or while studying.
Note

Avoid with epilepsy, and medicinal amounts in pregnancy.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Increasing forgetfulness affecting daily life, or a change in personality
  • Poor focus with persistent sadness or insomnia
  • Poor focus with tiredness and paleness (it may be anaemia or the thyroid)
