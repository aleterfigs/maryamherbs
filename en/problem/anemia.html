Anaemia and iron deficiency

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

The recipes here are iron-rich foods that help prevent and correct mild deficiency. A clear deficiency needs testing and treatment by a doctor. Do not take iron tablets without a test, especially if thalassaemia runs in the family.
First: make sure it is iron deficiency
  • Anaemia has many causes; iron deficiency is the commonest, but not the only one. A simple test (blood count and ferritin) finds the cause.
  • Thalassaemia trait is common in the Gulf and looks like iron deficiency on a blood count. Anyone with thalassaemia should take iron tablets only if a deficiency is confirmed by testing, because excess iron builds up and causes harm.
  • If the doctor prescribes iron tablets, food alone is usually not enough to correct the deficiency, but it helps prevent it coming back.
Where to get iron
  • Relatively strong Animal iron in red meat, liver, chicken and fish; the body absorbs it several times better than plant iron.
  • Relatively strong Plant iron in lentils, chickpeas, beans, spinach, dates, dried apricots and pumpkin seeds; it is absorbed better when eaten with vitamin C.
  • Relatively strong Vitamin C with the meal: a squeeze of lemon, an orange, tomatoes or peppers.
  • For pregnant women: liver is very rich in vitamin A, so a pregnant woman should not eat much of it; once a week in a small amount.
What reduces iron absorption
  • Relatively strong Tea and coffee with meals: they greatly reduce absorption of the meal's iron. Drink them at least an hour before or after eating. Tea after lunch is a common habit in our homes.
  • Milk, cheese and calcium supplements in the same meal.
  • If the doctor prescribes iron tablets: do not take them with tea or milk.
Myths about anaemia
  • «Spinach is the richest source of iron»: it contains iron, but its oxalates block absorption of most of it. Lentils and meat are more useful.
  • «Pomegranate and beetroot build blood»: they are red, but contain little iron.
  • «Date syrup cures anaemia»: it has a little iron and a lot of sugar. Eat it as food; it does not replace treatment.
  • «Herbs like moringa and nettle replace iron tablets»: they contain iron in lab analyses, but no good studies show they treat a deficiency.
Favism (G6PD deficiency)
  • It is common in the UAE and the Gulf, and is an inherited condition, different from iron deficiency.
  • Anyone with G6PD deficiency avoids fava beans in all forms, henna for children, and some medicines, as they can suddenly break down red blood cells.
  • Signs: yellowing and dark urine after eating fava beans. This needs a doctor immediately.

7 suitable blends

The Levant

Chicken livers with pomegranate molasses

A Levantine dish among the richest foods in iron, B12 and vitamin A.

Relatively strong evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Liver (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once a week.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For two people)
  • Liver: A quarter kilo of cleaned chicken livers
  • Garlic and chilli to taste
  • Pomegranate and its peel: One tablespoon of pomegranate molasses
  • Olives and olive oil: A spoon of olive oil
Method
  1. Fry the garlic in the oil, then add the livers and stir over a high heat until fully cooked.
  2. Add the pomegranate molasses, salt and pepper.
  3. Serve with bread and lemon.
Note

Pregnant women should not eat much liver, as it is rich in vitamin A. Cook it well.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Severe dizziness or fainting, or breathlessness and palpitations with slight effort
  • Black stools or blood in the stool or vomit: this may be bleeding in the stomach or bowel
  • A heavy period that soaks a pad every hour or lasts more than 7 days
  • A man, or a woman after menopause, with iron deficiency: needs a check of the digestive tract to find the source of bleeding
  • A pregnant woman with severe tiredness or paleness
  • A pale child, or one who eats soil, ice or clay
