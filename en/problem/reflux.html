Heartburn and acid reflux

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

Mint, vinegar and citrus can worsen heartburn, so we left them out of the heartburn recipes. The basics are weight, dinner timing and raising the head of the bed; the recipes are a help.
What really eases heartburn
  • Relatively strong Weight loss for anyone overweight, especially belly fat.
  • Moderate The last meal three hours before bed. In Ramadan: a moderate iftar, and suhoor a little earlier.
  • Moderate Raising the head of the bed by 15–20 cm with wooden blocks under the legs or a wedge pillow, for those with night-time heartburn. Ordinary pillows bend the stomach and do not help.
  • Moderate Sleeping on the left side.
  • Relatively strong Stopping smoking and shisha.
  • Smaller meals, and do not lie down straight after eating.
Common triggers, which vary from person to person
  • Fried and fatty food, and large meals.
  • Coffee and strong tea, chocolate, and fizzy drinks.
  • Mint: it relaxes the valve between the oesophagus and stomach, so it may worsen heartburn despite its reputation for the stomach.
  • Citrus and tomatoes, spicy food, and raw onion and garlic.
  • Do not cut out all of this: notice what triggers you and avoid only that.
Herbs and heartburn
  • Limited Chamomile: its infusion is a traditional stomach soother, but the evidence for reflux is limited.
  • Limited Marshmallow root: slimy and coats the throat and stomach; the European Medicines Agency recognises its traditional use for mild stomach irritation.
  • Limited Deglycyrrhizinated liquorice (DGL): a special preparation. Ordinary liquorice and the Ramadan liquorice drink raise blood pressure if taken in excess.
  • Limited Aloe vera: one small trial of a purified inner-gel syrup. Do not drink the yellow sap or the whole leaf; they are laxative and harmful.
  • Ginger: a little with food is fine; a lot may cause heartburn.
Myths about heartburn
  • «Cold milk puts out heartburn»: it soothes for a few minutes, then may stimulate the stomach to make more acid.
  • «Mint calms heartburn»: it may worsen it, as above.
  • «Apple cider vinegar cures reflux»: there is no evidence for this, and it is an acid that can burn an inflamed oesophagus.
  • «Baking soda every day»: fine once in a while, but it is high in sodium, and regular use harms blood pressure and the kidneys.
  • «Constant heartburn is normal»: if it happens more than twice a week, see a doctor, as chronic reflux can injure the oesophagus.

Common home recipes

Live yoghurt with banana and oats

Good bacteria with the fibre that feeds them, to help the gut recover.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once a day during treatment and for two weeks after.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • A pot of yoghurt with live cultures
  • A mashed banana
  • Oats: Two spoons of oats
Method
  1. Mix the ingredients and leave for 10 minutes for the oats to soften.
  2. Eat daily during the antibiotic and for two weeks after.
Note

Keep it two to three hours apart from the antibiotic dose, especially doxycycline and ciprofloxacin, because milk reduces their absorption.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Common home recipes

Oat and flaxseed porridge

A gentle breakfast for the colon: its soluble fibre regulates bowel movements and eases bloating.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Oats (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? As part of food, several times a week.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Oats: Half a cup of oats
  • Flaxseed (linseed): 1 tablespoon ground flaxseed
  • 1 cup water or lactose-free milk
  • Half a banana or one kiwi
Method
  1. Cook the oats in the water or milk for 5 minutes, stirring.
  2. Add the flaxseed after turning off the heat.
  3. Slice the fruit on top, and drink a glass of water afterwards.
Note

Start with half the amount for a week, then increase: fibre needs a gradual start and enough water.

Arab folk medicine

Chamomile infusion for heartburn

A warm, soothing cup for the stomach, without mint, because mint can make heartburn worse.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Chamomile (Moderate).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One to two cups a day.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Chamomile: 1 tablespoon dried chamomile flowers (about 2 g) or 1 tea bag
  • 1 cup boiling water (250 ml)
Method
  1. Put the chamomile in the cup and pour the boiling water over it.
  2. Cover for 5–10 minutes, then strain.
  3. Drink it warm, not hot, half an hour after eating or two hours before bed.
Note

Do not drink it very hot, as heat irritates the oesophagus. Avoid it if allergic to chamomile or daisies.

Arab folk medicine

Cold marshmallow root infusion for heartburn

Marshmallow root is slimy and coats the throat and stomach. It is soaked in cold water to release its mucilage.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? 1 to 3 cups a day, for two weeks, then review.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Marshmallow (khatmi): 1 teaspoon chopped marshmallow root (about 2 g)
  • 1 cup cold water (250 ml)
Method
  1. Put the root in the cold water and cover it.
  2. Leave it for two hours, stirring now and then.
  3. Strain it and drink it at room temperature.
Note

Keep it two hours apart from any medicine, as it may reduce absorption. For diabetics: it may lower blood sugar slightly.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Chest pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back, or with sweating and breathlessness: it may be the heart, not the stomach. Call an ambulance immediately
  • Difficulty or pain swallowing, or a feeling that food gets stuck
  • Vomiting blood or material like coffee grounds, or black stools
  • Unintended weight loss, or anaemia
  • Heartburn starting for the first time after age 55, or recurring more than twice a week for weeks
  • Chronic cough, hoarseness or night-time breathlessness without a clear cause
