Ear pain and infection in children

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

Put no oil, herb or milk in a child's ear before a doctor examines it. A warm compress on the outside is safe.
Most ear infections improve by themselves
  • Middle ear infection is very common in children, especially after a cold.
  • Most improve within 2–3 days, and the doctor decides whether an antibiotic is needed based on age and severity.
  • Signs in a young child: pulling at the ear, crying at night, fever and poor sleep.
What helps
  • Relatively strong Easing pain and fever with what the doctor or pharmacist recommends for the child's weight and age.
  • Moderate A warm (not hot) compress over the outside of the ear.
  • Moderate Fluids and rest, and raising the head slightly when an older child sleeps.
Prevention
  • Relatively strong No smoking or shisha around the child, including in the car.
  • Relatively strong Breastfeeding, and the child's vaccinations on time.
  • Moderate Do not bottle-feed the baby lying flat on its back; raise its head.
Do not put in a child's ear
  • Hot oil, garlic, onion, breast milk or herbal drops: if the eardrum is perforated they can cause harm and infection. Put nothing in the ear before a doctor has examined it.
  • Limited Olive oil drops with herbs eased pain in a small trial, but only after a doctor confirmed the eardrum was intact.
  • Cotton buds: they push wax deeper and can injure the canal or eardrum. The ear cleans itself.
Myths about the ear
  • «Breast milk in the ear cures infection»: there is no evidence, and it may carry germs into the ear canal.
  • «Cold air causes ear infections»: the cause is germs after a cold, not the air.
  • «Cautery behind the ear»: it burns the skin and leaves scars, and does not help.

Warm compress for ear pain

A simple outside compress that eases ear pain in adults and children, without putting anything inside the ear.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? 10–15 minutes, up to 3 times a day.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • A small clean towel
  • Warm (not hot) water
Method
  1. Soak the towel in the warm water and wring it out well.
  2. Test its warmth on the inside of your wrist first.
  3. Place it over the outside of the ear for 10–15 minutes.
  4. Repeat as needed.
Note

Do not use it hot, and put nothing inside the ear. For a baby under 6 months with pain or fever: see a doctor first.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Swelling or redness behind the ear, or the ear sticking out forward: an emergency
  • A baby under 6 months with ear pain or fever, or a fever of 39 or more at any age
  • Pus or blood coming from the ear, or severe pain that will not settle
  • A stiff neck, severe drowsiness or a severe headache
  • Hearing loss that persists after the infection, or repeated infections
  • A button battery in the ear or nose: an emergency, go immediately, and any other foreign object should be removed by a doctor
