Eczema and dry skin

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

The basics are plenty of moisturiser after bathing, and avoiding scented soap and hot water. The recipes here are for use on the skin.
What actually helps
  • Relatively strong Moisturiser, and plenty of it: at least twice a day, and within 3 minutes of bathing while the skin is still damp. Thick, unscented ones like petroleum jelly are best.
  • Relatively strong A short lukewarm bath 5–10 minutes, with a gentle unscented soap, patting dry rather than rubbing.
  • Moderate Soft cotton clothes, short nails, and cotton mittens for babies at night.
  • Moderate Common irritants here: dry air-conditioning, sweat in the heat, perfumes and incense, scented soap, wool and hot water.
Herbs and oils
  • Moderate Oatmeal bath: finely ground oats in bath water soothe itching, and it is recognised as a skin protectant.
  • Moderate Virgin coconut oil: in a trial on children, it improved eczema more than mineral oil.
  • Olive oil on a baby's skin: it may weaken the skin barrier, so it is not used as a daily moisturiser for babies.
  • Limited Evening primrose oil by mouth: a scientific review found it does not help eczema.
  • Unknown «natural» creams: some have been found to contain strong hidden cortisone that thins the skin.
Myths about eczema
  • «Eczema is contagious»: it does not spread by touch.
  • «It is always caused by food, so we cut many foods from the child's diet»: most of it is not caused by food, and cutting foods without testing harms the child's growth.
  • «Bathing harms eczema»: a short lukewarm bath followed by moisturiser helps.
  • «Lemon, vinegar or alcohol dries out eczema»: it burns the skin and makes it worse.

Oat bath for itching

Soothes the itch of eczema, chickenpox and insect bites.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Oats (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once a day when itchy.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Oats: A cup of very finely ground oats
  • A bath of lukewarm water
Method
  1. Grind the oats to a powder.
  2. Scatter them in the lukewarm bath water and stir.
  3. Soak for 15 minutes, then pat dry and apply a moisturiser.
Note

Careful: the bath becomes slippery.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Virgin coconut oil for eczema

In a trial on children with eczema, virgin coconut oil improved the skin more than mineral oil.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Twice a day.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • 1 teaspoon cold-pressed virgin coconut oil
Method
  1. After a lukewarm bath, pat the skin dry.
  2. Apply the oil while the skin is still damp, within 3 minutes.
  3. Repeat twice a day.
Note

Do not apply to broken skin or weeping, inflamed skin. Stop if redness increases. Avoid if allergic to coconut.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Skin that weeps, has yellow crusts or pus, or with fever: an infection that needs a doctor
  • A child with eczema who develops clustered painful blisters with fever: it may be a serious viral infection, see a doctor immediately
  • Itching that stops sleep or school
  • A baby with eczema and vomiting, diarrhoea or swelling after a particular food: it may be a food allergy
  • No improvement after two weeks of care
