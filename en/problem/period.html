Period pain

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

What really helps, in order of strength of evidence
  • Relatively strong Local heat on the lower abdomen or back.
  • Moderate Regular exercise and stretching; its effect builds over time.
  • Moderate Ginger on the first 3 days.
  • Moderate Fennel.
  • Limited Cinnamon, chamomile and omega-3 from fish.
Especially for teenage girls
  • Period pain is very common in the first years and usually eases with age.
  • Irregular periods are normal in the first two to three years after the first period.
  • Record your period dates and pain level in a calendar or app; this helps the doctor a lot.
  • Pain that makes you miss school is not something to bear in silence; tell your mother or the school nurse.
  • Eat iron-rich foods (meat, lentils, spinach, dates) with a source of vitamin C such as lemon.
  • Enough sleep, drinking water and cutting fizzy drinks and caffeine ease the symptoms.
What to avoid
  • Hormonal herbs for teenage girls, such as chasteberry and black cohosh.
  • Herbs that «bring on a period», such as pennyroyal and wormwood; dangerous and possibly poisonous.
  • Herbalists' blends with unknown ingredients.

Ginger powder on the first days of the period

One of the most studied herbs for period pain; trials in young women found it clearly eases the pain.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? 2–3 times a day on the first 3 days of the period only.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Ginger: A quarter to half a teaspoon of ground ginger
  • Honey: One teaspoon of honey or a cup of warm water
Method
  1. Mix the ginger with the honey or dissolve it in the warm water.
  2. Start on the first day of the period or a day before.
Note

May cause heartburn in those with a sensitive stomach. For teenagers: the smaller amount.

A warm compress for period pain

Heat on the lower belly is one of the most effective ways to ease the pain.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Whenever you need it on painful days.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • A hot-water bottle, heating pad or warm towel
  • Chamomile: A cup of warm chamomile to drink alongside (optional)
Method
  1. Fill the bottle with warm (not boiling) water and wrap it in a towel.
  2. Place it on the lower abdomen or lower back for 20 minutes.
  3. Drink the warm chamomile and lie in a comfortable position.
Note

Check the temperature to avoid burns, and do not fall asleep with a hot bottle on your skin.

Stretches for period pain

Gentle movements that relax the abdominal and pelvic muscles; regular exercise reduces pain over time.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Daily, and twice a day on painful days.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • A mat or rug
  • 10 minutes
Method
  1. Child's pose: kneel, stretch your arms in front with your forehead on the floor, for a minute.
  2. Cat and cow: on hands and knees, arch your back up then down slowly, 10 times.
  3. Lie on your back and hug your knees to your chest, for a minute.
  4. Walk 20–30 minutes on most days of the month.
Note

Avoid strenuous exercise if the pain increases.

Ingredients (For one cup)

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Pain so bad it stops you studying or working: it is not normal to put up with it, and a doctor can help
  • Pain outside your period days, pain when opening your bowels, or pain that gets worse year after year (it may be endometriosis)
  • Sudden severe pain, or pain when you might be pregnant
  • Very heavy bleeding (changing your pad every hour)
  • Pain with fever or unusual discharge
