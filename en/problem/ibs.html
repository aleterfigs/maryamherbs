Irritable bowel

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

IBS is chronic, it calms and flares, and it does not turn into cancer. The remedies here ease symptoms; the foundation is regular meals, soluble fibre and knowing your own triggers.

Common home recipes

After-meal digestive tea

For bloating, wind and a heavy stomach after a rich meal.

Relatively strong evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Mint (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One cup once or twice a day; can be continued 4–6 weeks, then a week's break.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Mint: One teaspoon of dried mint or fresh leaves
  • Fennel: Half a teaspoon of crushed fennel seeds
  • Anise: Half a teaspoon of crushed anise seeds
Method
  1. Put the ingredients in a small teapot.
  2. Pour over a cup of boiling water and cover.
  3. Leave for 10 minutes, then strain and drink warm.
Note

People with reflux can replace the mint with chamomile.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Okayu (rice porridge with ginger)

The food of the sick in Japan, very light on the stomach.

Relatively strong evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Ginger (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? As part of food, several times a week.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one bowl)
  • A quarter cup of rice
  • Two and a half cups of water
  • Ginger: A small slice of ginger
Method
  1. Rinse the rice and cook it with the water and ginger over a very low heat for 40 minutes, stirring now and then.
  2. Remove the ginger and add a little salt.
  3. Serve warm in small amounts.
Note

Suitable after vomiting or diarrhoea, with plenty of fluids.

Psyllium for diarrhoea and constipation

The same fibre regulates stools in both cases.

  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Classical medical books.

How much and when? Once or twice a day with plenty of water; can be continued for long periods.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Psyllium (ispaghula): One teaspoon of psyllium husk
  • A full cup of water
Method
  1. Mix the psyllium with the water and stir well.
  2. Drink at once before it sets, then another cup of water.
  3. Once or twice a day.
Note

Never take without enough water, and keep two hours apart from medicines.

Oat and flaxseed porridge

A gentle breakfast for the colon: its soluble fibre regulates bowel movements and eases bloating.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Oats (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? As part of food, several times a week.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Oats: Half a cup of oats
  • Flaxseed (linseed): 1 tablespoon ground flaxseed
  • 1 cup water or lactose-free milk
  • Half a banana or one kiwi
Method
  1. Cook the oats in the water or milk for 5 minutes, stirring.
  2. Add the flaxseed after turning off the heat.
  3. Slice the fruit on top, and drink a glass of water afterwards.
Note

Start with half the amount for a week, then increase: fibre needs a gradual start and enough water.

India (Ayurveda)

Kanji (Kerala rice gruel)

Kerala's food for convalescence and an upset stomach: rice cooked in plenty of water and eaten with its liquid.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Ginger (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One cup 2–3 times a day until the symptoms go, for a week at most; if no better, see a doctor.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • A quarter cup of rice
  • 4 cups of water
  • A pinch of salt
  • Ginger: A slice of ginger (optional)
Method
  1. Wash the rice well.
  2. Cook it in the water over low heat for 30–40 minutes until soft, with plenty of liquid left.
  3. Add the salt and eat it warm with its liquid.
Note

With severe diarrhoea, start with oral rehydration solution first, then kanji. For children, leave out the ginger.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Blood in the stool or black stool, or anaemia
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Symptoms starting for the first time after age 40, or a lasting change in bowel habit
  • Diarrhoea that wakes you from sleep, or fever
  • A family history of colon cancer, inflammatory bowel disease or coeliac disease
  • Women over 50: persistent, almost daily bloating or feeling full quickly needs an ovarian check
  • Severe persistent pain, repeated vomiting, or bloating with no passing of wind or stool: emergency now
