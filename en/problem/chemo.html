Supporting a cancer patient during treatment

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

These recipes support nutrition and ease symptoms; they are not a treatment for cancer. Do not take any herb or supplement during treatment without the oncology team's approval. Choose «Cancer being treated now» under «For more accurate results» so the helper rules out anything that may conflict with treatment.
The basic rule
  • Tell the oncology team about every herb, supplement or herbal drink before taking it, and carry a list for them. Some herbs reduce the effect of chemotherapy or increase its toxicity.
  • The aim of food during treatment: keeping weight, strength and fluids — not «curing cancer».
  • Small meals every 2–3 hours are better than three large ones.
Common symptoms and what helps
  • Nausea: cold, dry foods, mild ginger with the team's approval, and avoiding strong smells and fatty food.
  • Mouth sores: salt and bicarbonate rinse, soft food that is not spicy or sour, and a soft brush.
  • Taste changes: plastic spoons for a metallic taste, light seasoning with herbs, and cold foods.
  • Poor appetite: calorie-rich foods in small amounts: yoghurt, dates, tahini, soup and smoothies.
  • Diarrhoea: fluids and rehydration solution, rice water, bananas and rice, and avoiding coarse fibre and dairy if they make it worse.
  • Constipation: fluids and movement, and fibre if the team allows.
  • Fatigue: protein at every meal, and a light daily walk if possible.
Herbs and supplements to avoid during treatment
  • St John's wort: cancels the effect of many chemotherapy drugs.
  • High-dose turmeric and curcumin supplements, and green-tea extract: may interact with some treatment drugs. Turmeric in food and ordinary tea are fine in moderation.
  • Ginseng, echinacea, ashwagandha and elderberry: affect immunity and medicines.
  • Grapefruit, bitter orange and lots of pomegranate juice: may change the level of some medicines in the blood.
  • Large amounts of garlic, ginger, ginkgo and fish oil: increase bleeding when platelets are low.
  • Hormonal herbs (chasteberry, cohosh, liquorice, and lots of anise and fennel) with hormone-sensitive cancers such as some breast cancers.
  • «Alternative cancer cures» such as bitter almonds, apricot kernels, oleander and harmal: poisonous and can kill.
  • High doses of vitamins and antioxidants without the doctor's approval.
Food safety with low immunity
  • Cook meat, chicken and eggs thoroughly, and do not eat raw or half-cooked food.
  • Milk and dairy pasteurised only, including camel milk.
  • Wash fruit and vegetables well, and peel them with very low immunity.
  • Avoid raw sprouts, open buffets and food left out of the fridge.
  • Unsterilised dried herbs may carry moulds; buy from a trusted source and steep in boiling water.
  • The team may ask you to avoid raw, unpasteurised honey with very low immunity.

Common home recipes

Light ginger water for treatment nausea

According to some trials, ginger alongside anti-sickness medicines may ease chemotherapy nausea.

Relatively strong evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Ginger (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Sips through the day on nausea days, up to two cups a day.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Ginger: Two thin slices of fresh ginger
  • A cup of warm water
  • A slice of lemon (optional)
Method
  1. Steep the ginger in boiling water for 5 minutes, then remove it.
  2. Sip slowly, lukewarm or slightly cool.
Note

Take only with the treating team's approval; avoid if platelets are low or with blood thinners. It does not replace anti-sickness medicines.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Salt and bicarbonate mouth rinse for mouth sores

The rinse recommended by many cancer centres to clean the mouth and ease sores.

Traditional, no studiesSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: There are no studies on its ingredients for this problem; it relies on traditional use.
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? After meals and before bed, 4–6 times a day.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • A quarter teaspoon of salt
  • A quarter teaspoon of sodium bicarbonate
  • A cup of warm water (boiled then cooled)
Method
  1. Dissolve the salt and bicarbonate in the water.
  2. Rinse gently for 30 seconds, then spit out.
  3. Make a fresh batch every day.
Note

Do not use mouthwashes containing alcohol. Use a very soft toothbrush.

Prophetic medicine

Talbina

Barley porridge with milk and honey, for the sick, the sad and the tired.

Moderate evidenceNeeds caution for some groupsAuthentic (sahih) hadith
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Barley (Moderate).
  • Safety: Barley: not suitable for some situations (pregnancy, breastfeeding, illnesses or medicines). Use «Suggest a blend» to find out whether it suits you.
  • Source: Authentic (sahih) hadith.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
Authentic (sahih) hadith«Talbina gives rest to the heart of the sick and takes away some of the grief” (Bukhari, from Aisha, may God be pleased with her). «Gives rest» means it soothes and revives.
How much and when? A bowl when ill, sad or tired, or several times a week.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Barley: Two tablespoons of barley flour (wholegrain preferred)
  • One and a half cups of milk or water
  • Honey: One teaspoon of honey
Method
  1. Mix the barley flour with a little cold milk so it does not go lumpy.
  2. Add the rest of the milk and heat over a low heat, stirring, for 10–15 minutes until it is like a soup.
  3. Take off the heat and sweeten with honey once lukewarm.
Note

Not suitable for people with coeliac disease. For children under one, no honey.

Easy to swallow, rich in protein and fluids, suitable when eating is reduced.

  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.

How much and when? Small, frequent meals.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For four people)
  • Chicken and chicken broth: A chicken breast
  • A carrot, a courgette and a potato
  • A small onion
  • 5 cups of water
  • Cumin: A light pinch of cumin
Method
  1. Cook the chicken and vegetables in the water until completely soft (40 minutes).
  2. Blend until smooth.
  3. Serve warm, not hot, in small frequent amounts.
Note

Cook well and eat fresh; do not leave out of the fridge.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • A temperature of 38 °C or more during chemotherapy: emergency immediately; it may be a serious infection with low immunity
  • Unusual bleeding or a lot of bruising
  • Vomiting that prevents drinking fluids for more than a day
  • Diarrhoea more than 4–6 times a day, or with dizziness and dehydration
  • Mouth sores that stop eating and drinking
  • Shortness of breath, chest pain or confusion
