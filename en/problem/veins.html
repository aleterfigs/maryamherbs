Varicose veins and swollen legs

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

No herb removes varicose veins. The basics: compression stockings, raising the legs and walking. The recipes here are soothing compresses, and fibre to prevent the constipation that increases pressure on the veins.
What really helps
  • Relatively strong Medical compression stockings in the right size, put on in the morning.
  • Relatively strong Raising the legs above heart level for 15–30 minutes, three times a day.
  • Relatively strong Walking and calf exercises: raising and lowering the heels pumps blood back to the heart.
  • Moderate Do not stand or sit for long without moving. On long journeys: move your feet every half hour, walk the aisle and drink water.
  • Moderate Losing weight, and avoiding constipation, intense heat and long hot baths, as heat widens the veins.
Herbs and varicose veins
  • Moderate Horse chestnut (standardised extract): a Cochrane review found it eases pain, swelling and itching in chronic venous insufficiency in the short term. Only as a pharmacy product, as the raw seeds are poisonous; not with blood thinners, and not in pregnancy.
  • Limited Witch hazel: cold astringent compresses soothe the heavy feeling.
  • No herb or cream removes visible varicose veins; their definitive treatment is with a vascular doctor.
Myths about varicose veins
  • «Varicose veins are only cosmetic»: they can cause pain, swelling, skin changes and ulcers if neglected.
  • «A cream dissolves varicose veins»: there is none.
  • «Exercise harms varicose veins»: the opposite; walking is one of the most useful things.
  • «Crossing your legs causes varicose veins»: there is no strong evidence; long standing and sitting and heredity matter more.

Psyllium for diarrhoea and constipation

The same fibre regulates stools in both cases.

Relatively strong evidenceSafe for most peopleClassical medical books
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Psyllium (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Classical medical books.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once or twice a day with plenty of water; can be continued for long periods.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Psyllium (ispaghula): One teaspoon of psyllium husk
  • A full cup of water
Method
  1. Mix the psyllium with the water and stir well.
  2. Drink at once before it sets, then another cup of water.
  3. Once or twice a day.
Note

Never take without enough water, and keep two hours apart from medicines.

A breakfast that lowers cholesterol and eases the bowels.

How much and when? As part of food, several times a week.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Oats: Half a cup of oats
  • Flaxseed (linseed): One teaspoon of ground flax
  • Dates and fresh dates (ajwa): Two chopped dates
  • A cup of milk or water
Method
  1. Cook the oats in the milk for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the flax and dates.
  3. Serve warm.
Note

For diabetics: one date.

How much and when? Once or twice a day when needed.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Witch hazel (hamamelis): Witch-hazel water or ready-made wipes
  • Cotton wool
Method
  1. Soak the cotton in cold witch-hazel water.
  2. Place it gently on the area for a few minutes.
  3. Repeat after using the toilet.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • One leg suddenly swollen, painful and warm, especially after a long journey, surgery or childbirth: it may be a clot, an emergency
  • Sudden breathlessness or chest pain with a swollen leg: an emergency, go immediately
  • Both legs swollen with breathlessness or little urine: it may come from the heart or kidneys
  • A wound on the leg that does not heal, or darkened, hardened skin near the ankle
  • Bleeding from a varicose vein: press on it, raise the leg and get help
  • A red, hard, painful varicose vein
