Migraine

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

Migraine is a neurological illness with effective preventive treatment from a doctor if attacks recur. The recipes here are a help; the basics are knowing your triggers and regular sleep, meals and water.
What really reduces attacks
  • Relatively strong A headache diary: write down when each attack happens and what came before it, to learn your own triggers.
  • Relatively strong Regular sleep and meals: too little or too much sleep and delayed meals are among the commonest triggers.
  • Moderate Water: dehydration in the Gulf heat often triggers migraine.
  • Moderate In Ramadan: fasting headaches usually come from missing coffee, hunger and dehydration. Cut coffee gradually two weeks before Ramadan, eat suhoor, and drink water between iftar and suhoor.
  • Moderate Regular exercise and less stress.
  • During an attack: a dark, quiet room, a cold compress on the forehead or neck, and sleep if possible.
Common triggers
  • Lack of sleep, delayed meals, dehydration and heat.
  • Bright light and screens, and noise.
  • Strong perfumes, incense and oud.
  • Hormonal changes with the period.
  • Stress, and sometimes the sudden rest after it (weekend headache).
  • For some people: aged cheeses, chocolate and processed meats. Do not cut out a food unless you have found that it triggers you.
Herbs and supplements
  • Moderate Magnesium and vitamin B2 (riboflavin) for prevention: both have trials showing fewer attacks. Ask the doctor about the dose, especially with kidney disease.
  • Limited Ginger: in a small trial, the powder taken at the start of an attack eased pain and nausea.
  • Moderate Diluted peppermint oil on the forehead: it has trials in tension headache more than in migraine.
  • Limited Feverfew: trial results are conflicting, and it is avoided in pregnancy and with blood thinners.
  • Butterbur: it helped in trials, but most products contain substances that harm the liver; use only a product labelled free of them, under a doctor's supervision.
Myths about migraine
  • «Migraine is just a bad headache»: it is a neurological illness with attacks, nausea and light sensitivity, and doctors have effective preventive treatment.
  • «Cauterising the head stops migraine»: it leaves burns, scars and infections, and there is no evidence that it works.
  • «Coffee is always harmful»: a little may ease an attack, but a lot, or stopping suddenly, triggers it.
  • «The more painkillers, the more relief»: painkiller overuse causes chronic daily headache.

Diluted peppermint oil for headache

For tension headache, rubbed lightly on the forehead and temples.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Mint (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? When you have a headache; can be repeated after 15 minutes, up to 3 times a day.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Mint: Two drops of peppermint essential oil
  • One teaspoon of olive or coconut oil
Method
  1. Mix the essential oil with the carrier oil.
  2. Rub gently on the forehead and temples.
  3. Repeat after 15 minutes if needed.
Note

Keep away from the eyes. Not for young children, and never on a baby's face.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Lavender compress for headache

A cold compress and calming scent for tension headache.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Lavender (Moderate).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once or twice a day when needed.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Lavender: One tablespoon of lavender flowers
  • A cup of boiling water
  • A small towel
Method
  1. Steep the lavender for 10 minutes, then cool the water.
  2. Soak the towel and wring it out.
  3. Place it on the forehead for 15 minutes in a quiet room.
Note

If the headache is severe and sudden, or comes with numbness, go to the emergency department.

Ground ginger at the start of a migraine

In a small trial, ground ginger taken at the start of an attack eased pain and nausea.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Ginger (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once at an attack, may be repeated after 4 hours, twice a day at most.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Ginger: ⅛ teaspoon ground ginger (about 250 mg)
  • A cup of warm water
Method
  1. Mix the ginger into the warm water.
  2. Drink it at the first signs of an attack.
  3. Lie down in a dark, quiet room.
Note

It does not replace treatment your doctor prescribes. Avoid large amounts with blood thinners. If attacks happen more than 4 times a month, see a doctor about preventive treatment.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • The worst headache of your life, starting suddenly like a thunderclap: an emergency, go immediately
  • Headache with weakness or numbness on one side of the body, or trouble speaking or seeing for the first time: an emergency
  • A new headache after age fifty, or one that wakes you from sleep and worsens with coughing
  • A headache after a blow to the head
  • Taking painkillers on more than 10 days a month: this can turn into a chronic headache from painkiller overuse
  • In pregnancy: severe headache with swelling or vision problems
