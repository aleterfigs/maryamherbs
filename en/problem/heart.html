Heart health

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

Food for the heart
  • Relatively strong The Mediterranean diet: olive oil, vegetables, pulses, fish twice a week and nuts.
  • Relatively strong Cutting salt, sugar and trans fats (commercial fried food and factory pastries).
  • Moderate Fibre from oats, barley and psyllium lowers cholesterol.
  • Quitting smoking and shisha is one of the most important things you can do for your heart.
Herbs and heart medicines
  • Many heart medicines interact with herbs: blood thinners, digoxin and blood-pressure medicines. Add your medicines under «For more accurate results» so the helper rules out anything that conflicts with them.
  • Hawthorn is used for the heart in Europe, but it doubles heart medicines; use only under supervision.
  • Liquorice, bitter orange and yohimbe are dangerous for heart patients.

A salt-reduction plan using spices

Cutting salt is one of the strongest ways to lower blood pressure. Spices and lemon make up for the taste.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleModern home recipe
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Garlic (Moderate).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Modern home recipe.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Daily, as a way of life.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Sumac: Sumac
  • Cumin: Cumin
  • Garlic: Garlic
  • Lemon, black pepper and fresh herbs
Method
  1. Remove the salt shaker from the table, and cut salt in cooking gradually over two weeks — your taste adapts.
  2. Use lemon, sumac, garlic and cumin instead of salt.
  3. Cut down on pickles, salty cheeses, tinned foods, crisps and stock cubes.
  4. Read labels: the goal is under 5 grams of salt a day (one teaspoon) from all sources.
Note

Do not use salt substitutes with kidney disease or potassium-sparing BP medicines without asking the doctor.

Sencha green tea

Japan's everyday tea, made with water below boiling.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Green tea (Moderate).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? One to two cups a day; not in the evening.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • Green tea (matcha and sencha): One teaspoon of sencha
  • A cup of water at about 75 °C
Method
  1. Boil the water and let it cool for two minutes.
  2. Steep the tea for one and a half to two minutes only.
  3. Strain; the same leaves can be steeped a second time.
Note

Contains caffeine: not in the evening, and drink between meals, not with them.

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • Pain or pressure in the chest spreading to the arm, jaw or back, with sweating or shortness of breath: call an ambulance immediately
  • Shortness of breath on slight exertion or when lying down
  • Swollen legs
  • Palpitations with dizziness or fainting
  • Signs of stroke: face drooping, arm weakness, difficulty speaking: emergency immediately
