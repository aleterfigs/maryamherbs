Bones and vitamin D deficiency

These results are for a non-pregnant adult without chronic illness or medicines. In the app you choose your age, condition and medicines to see the blends that suit you.

No herb builds bones. The basics: vitamin D after a test, calcium from food, and walking and exercise. The recipes here are calcium-rich foods.
Vitamin D in a sunny country
  • Vitamin D deficiency is very common in the UAE and the Gulf despite the sun: air-conditioned homes and cars, covering clothes, and darker skin needs longer.
  • Relatively strong A simple blood test shows your level, and the doctor decides on the supplement and dose. Too much is harmful, so do not take high doses without a test.
  • Moderate The sun: 10–20 minutes on the arms and legs or the face and hands, several times a week. In summer: early morning or shortly before sunset to avoid the heat. Sun through glass is not enough, as glass blocks the rays that make vitamin D.
  • From food: sardines, salmon and tuna, egg yolks, and fortified milk and laban. Food alone is usually not enough for someone who is deficient.
Calcium and bones
  • Relatively strong Calcium from food first: milk, laban and cheese, sardines with their bones, tahini and sesame, almonds, leafy greens and dried figs.
  • Relatively strong Walking and weight-bearing and resistance exercise strengthen bones, and balance exercises prevent falls.
  • Moderate Cut down on salt and fizzy drinks, and stop smoking and shisha.
  • For older people: good lighting at night, removing slippery rugs, and grab bars in the bathroom, because falls are what break fragile bones.
Myths about bones
  • «Bone broth is rich in calcium»: it has very little calcium; a cup of laban is far more useful.
  • «I do not need vitamin D because I live in a sunny country»: most people tested in the Gulf are deficient.
  • «Osteoporosis is only for older women»: it affects men too, and bone is built from childhood until about thirty.
  • «Herbs build bones»: no herb has been shown to treat osteoporosis. Unknown supplements may contain cortisone, which weakens bones further.

6 suitable blends

The Maghreb

Sardine salad with lemon and cumin

A small, cheap fish rich in vitamin D, calcium (from its bones), omega-3 and B12.

Moderate evidenceSafe for most peopleInherited folk custom
  • Evidence: Strongest ingredient for this problem: Olives and olive oil (Relatively strong).
  • Safety: No common contraindications are known for its ingredients at the stated amounts.
  • Source: Inherited folk custom.
  • An estimated rating based on the blend's ingredients, since the blend itself has usually not been tested as a combination.
How much and when? Once or twice a week.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For one cup)
  • A tin of sardines in olive oil or water (with bones)
  • Cumin: A pinch of cumin
  • Juice of a lemon, spring onion and parsley
  • Olives and olive oil: A spoon of olive oil
Method
  1. Open the tin and mash the sardines with their soft bones using a fork.
  2. Add the lemon, cumin, onion, parsley and oil.
  3. Eat with bread or salad.
Note

Tinned sardines are salty; people with high blood pressure should choose low-salt.

🌿 From traditional medicine; most of its recipes have not been tested in large studies. Choose organic or clean local ingredients from a trusted source where you can, and if you do not improve, see a doctor.

Balls of tahini, dates and honey — an energy-rich snack.

  • Source: Inherited folk custom.

How much and when? Two to three balls a day between meals.
Ingredients and method
Ingredients (For twenty balls)
  • Dates and fresh dates (ajwa): A cup of pitted dates
  • A quarter cup of tahini
  • Honey: A spoon of honey
  • Toasted sesame for coating
Method
  1. Mash the dates with the tahini and honey into a paste.
  2. Roll into small balls and coat in sesame.
  3. Keeps in the fridge for a week.
Note

High in sugar. Avoid with sesame allergy. No honey under one year.

A smooth dessert, easy to swallow and rich in calories.

Ingredients (For three bowls)

See a doctor instead of herbs if:

  • A fracture from a minor fall or with no clear cause: a sign of osteoporosis that needs a bone density scan
  • Sudden severe back pain, loss of height, or a stooped back
  • Widespread bone pain with muscle weakness and difficulty climbing stairs: it may be severe vitamin D deficiency
  • A woman after menopause, or anyone taking cortisone tablets for months: ask the doctor about a density scan
